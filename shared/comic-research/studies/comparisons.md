# Contrasting comic approaches

25 actual comics visually inspected: 11 Peanuts, 7 Poorly Drawn Lines, 7 The Far Side.

## Evidence and limits

Opened official creator/museum/publisher pages in the cloud browser, read source labels, resolved their actual artwork URLs from page DOM, opened the artwork and visually inspected browser screenshots. Each counted record was visually inspected, not inferred from title, alt text, summary, or search snippet. The Far Side captions were read on the official page in conjunction with its directly inspected image.

Purposive contrast sample, not a representative or random survey. Peanuts examples were selected across 1950–1996 from a museum exhibition; Poorly Drawn Lines examples from the creator archive span 2025–2026; The Far Side examples come from two current official Daily Dose selections of older work.

Motives, mechanisms, themes, and lessons are the researcher’s interpretation of the observed comics, not claims of the creators’ stated intent.

All selected official pages loaded without account setup or payment. No access denial was encountered. No copied artwork is included in this analysis deliverable; source image URLs are provenance references only. Three browser screenshots are held separately for private integration QA, not public redistribution. The Far Side page displays a restrictive reproduction notice, respected by linking and providing original analysis rather than reproducing artwork or complete captions.

These are established named comic bodies of work chosen for craft contrast, not ranked by popularity. No audience size, engagement metric, or causal claim about popularity is made.

## Why each complements the others

### Peanuts
Offers relationship-specific beliefs, hesitation, withdrawal, quiet recognition, purposeful stillness, and a seven-panel internal argument. It demonstrates how small everyday situations can support character causality without fantasy spectacle.

Limit: Museum-curated dailies, not a representative Sunday/era sample. Ten examples use four panels; one uses seven. No fully wordless complete strip was counted: PN-05 has no dialogue but depends on a sign; PN-03 and PN-10 contain silent panels.

### Poorly Drawn Lines
Offers contemporary deadpan delivery, minimalist expressions, social awkwardness, cross-category absurdity, and unequal/square/centered-tail layouts. PDL-01 adds a reflective ending that need not resolve as a conventional gag.

Limit: Small recent creator-archive sample. Several examples rely on English idiom, internet discourse, profanity, or abrupt dark incongruity; these are observations, not requirements for Dotter. No fully wordless example in this selection.

### The Far Side
Offers single-panel compression, reader inference, caption-image mismatch, role reversal, visible consequences, and scale changes. It counterbalances sequential character comedy by testing whether a premise can be understood in one image.

Limit: Only two official web-selection days. These are re-presentations of older cartoons; original publication dates were not verified. Some premises use dark violence, stereotypes, or culturally specific references unsuitable for direct transfer.

## Practical synthesis

- Start from a character’s desired outcome or mistaken belief. A reaction should follow that belief rather than serve as an interchangeable punchline.
- Choose the minimum visual evidence that lets the reader reconstruct causality: ground line, returned object, posture, prop, count, scale, or changed rendering.
- Separate the reveal from the response when the response changes the relationship. PDL-02 and PN-07 gain a second layer from what happens after a joke or error.
- Use silence precisely. A silent panel can register thought or demonstrate a fact; a sign-only comic is not the same as a wordless comic.
- Make panel geometry answer the story: repeated narrow frames for eroding certainty, an isolated visual surprise, a compressed lifetime, or one dense tableau.
- Do not force a universal moral. PN-06 creates an acquaintance through an action; PDL-01 ends on a modest aspiration; other examples simply expose a flawed rule.
- Transfer mechanisms only after replacing premise, roles, props, phrasing, composition, and visual identity. A new costume on the same joke is not original research use.

- Setup: The child bends toward his dog as though leaving for a substantial separation.
- Motive or belief: Charlie Brown experiences a small separation with outsized emotional seriousness.
