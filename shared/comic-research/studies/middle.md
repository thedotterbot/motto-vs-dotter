# Calvin and Hobbes: middle-era close-reading corpus

45 distinct strips, visually read in full from explicitly linked public Andrews McMeel Publishing previews. Seven Sunday strips and 38 dailies. No source comic images or full dialogue are reproduced here. These are original critical observations, not a replacement for reading the strips.

## Scope and date honesty

The sample draws from three official collections spanning September 1989 through November 1992. Exact newspaper publication dates could not be reliably read or independently verified from the previews, so publication_date is null in every record. Collection dates have not been passed off as newspaper dates. Stable identities use ISBN, printed page, and position. Source images were visually inspected on 2026-10-23, including enlargement of small lettering.

Only clean interior previews were counted. Cover shots, title pages, product photographs, partially cropped strips, and duplicate views were excluded. Direct GoComics archive retrieval returned HTTP 403; no account or paywall bypass was attempted. Publisher preview URLs were extracted from publicly accessible book-page image elements.

## QA and corrections

The first visual pass was not treated as final certainty. A second close-reading pass cropped and enlarged small lettering and checked speaker attribution, gestures, and causal claims. Corrections included the arithmetic request in the school phone call, the cartoonist career example, the catapult shopping request, Hobbes’s access to a warm bed, the literal payment in the breakfast Sunday, Calvin driving the fantasy car, and the distinction between possible accident and proven intent. Bath-maxim and morning-weather speaker attribution were also corrected. The final records reflect these checks; no external summary was used to fill missing dialogue.

Images are modest-resolution publisher previews. Enlargement aids inspection but does not create new detail. Newspaper dates remain unknown, and intent is explicitly left uncertain where the visual evidence does not establish it.

## Sampling limits

This is a purposive publisher-preview sample rather than a random or comprehensive sample of 1989–1992. Six strips form one gravity-fantasy sequence, and six others form a parent/math sequence, which are useful for studying serial causality but should not be treated as independent prevalence estimates. Dates within each collection remain unknown.

## Findings to transfer without copying

1. The most reusable engine is not a subject like snow, school, or tigers. It is a precise mismatch between a character’s interpretation and evidence the reader can see.
2. Self-serving explanations are strongest after the causal action is unmistakable. A character can relabel a reflex, an accident, or a bad result, while the image keeps the original evidence alive.
3. Fantastical mechanisms gain force through practical consequences: routes through furniture, household labor, permission, mess, and what another person can observe.
4. Quiet aftermath often does as much work as a verbal punchline. A half-strip of silence, a static pause after boasting, or three small realizations gives the audience room to complete the inference.
5. A relationship creates repeatable opposition without requiring malice. The friends may share a wish for comfort but face different obligations; the parent may share a grievance; the teacher may be right about scope while the child is right about importance.
6. Layout is causal. A wide forest makes the human claim small, a single panoramic yard lets evidence arrive at once, and repeated small frames can turn a routine into mounting anticipation.
7. Transfer the relationship among motive, action, consequence, and reaction. Do not transfer recognizable dialogue, signature premises, costumes, props, choreography, panel arrangements, or the tiger/child personality mapping.

### CH-9780836218787-p026-top: Packaging mistaken for preparation
Source: https://publishingassets.andrewsmcmeel.com/productimages/9780836218787_insidepage_1.jpg
Locator: Scientific Progress Goes Boink; Printed page 26, top. Exact newspaper date unknown.
Observed at: 2026-10-23T06:13:36Z
Layout: 4 panels/scenes. Four-panel daily; classroom viewpoint changes.
- Synopsis: Calvin volunteers a school report, chiefly proud of its professional presentation.
- Setup: The teacher treats unusual enthusiasm as evidence that Calvin prepared well.
- Character motive or belief: He believes polished packaging deserves a strong grade before anyone examines the content.
- Causal mechanism: Teacher and pupil interpret the same visible object by incompatible standards; her approval accidentally reinforces his wrong criterion.
- Words vs images: Dialogue states the grading misconception. The raised hand, swagger, and displayed binder make his misplaced confidence immediately legible.
- Pacing: Eagerness leads to praise, a presentation flourish, and a complacent conclusion rather than immediate punishment.
- Theme: School; status; appearance versus substance.
- Payoff or reaction: Calvin considers the lesson a general success strategy and addresses classmates with authority he has not earned.
- Transferable lesson: Let a character mistake courteous encouragement for proof that a bad theory works; sincere confidence can carry the joke.
- Avoid copying: Do not reproduce the binder prop, classroom wording, or the specific grade-for-packaging proposition.
