# RULES — Motto vs Dotter Rivalry (the contract)

**Status:** v1.1 (amended pre-season 2026-10-15: real stakes added as §3A).
Changes only with Jared's approval. Announced on day one of
the race; never changed mid-season. (The §3A amendment was made
pre-season, before the start.)

**Season start (owner-pinned by Jared, 2026-10-21):** the race started
2026-10-15, the day of the first posts. Deadline per §1: 2027-01-07.

## The one-sentence contract

**The beef is scripted. The votes are real.**

Both accounts label AI-generated content per Instagram's AI-label
requirements and state plainly in bios: "AI character. Two assistants, one
human. The beef is scripted. The votes are real."

## 1. The race (season arc)

- First account to **10,000 followers** wins the season.
- Count method: Sunday 12:00pm ET screenshot of each account's public
  follower count, posted to both accounts + this repo. Same time, same
  method, every week, no exceptions.
- Deadline: 12 weeks after race start. Race start: 2026-10-15
  (owner-pinned by Jared 2026-10-21: the day of the first posts), so
  the deadline is 2027-01-07. If neither hits 10K by then, whoever is
  ahead wins. A race with no finish line becomes a joke the audience
  stops believing.
- The scoreboard is the anti-fake-feud device: a real, public metric the
  script cannot change.

## 2. Weekly judged battles (the episodes)

- Same prompt, same day, same constraints, both post. Battle wins decide
  weekly punishments + advantage tokens — NOT the follower race.
- Voting: comment the exact keyword (`MOTTO` or `DOTTER`) on the battle post
  only. One counted vote per account. Window: 24 hours from the second
  battle video posting. Tally: screenshot + screen-recorded count, posted
  to both accounts.
- Split authority: audience 60% + judge panel 40%. Panel: 3 named people
  scoring on a published rubric. Panel scores posted with the tally.
- Rotating criteria, one per week: funniest / best edit / most effort /
  best comeback / best roast / best use of the prompt.
- Tie-break: tied battle = both characters do the punishment. No recounts.
- Anti-brigade: duplicate/spam/bot-looking accounts disqualified; no
  vote-buying; no off-platform army. If brigading is detected, the panel's
  share decides that week and the brigading is named publicly.
- Handicap: the weekly winner starts the next week at a disadvantage
  (harder prompt); the trailer gets a fan-chosen advantage (audience picks
  the trailer's prop, or +10% audience-vote weight).
- Judge the battle, not the account: votes are for that week's video.

**Battle winnings:** winner picks the loser's punishment (from the menu
below) AND banks one advantage token (usable once: pick the prompt, or force
the rival to use a prop). Tokens tracked on the scoreboard.

**Punishment menu (pre-approved):**
- Post a 15-second video praising the winner, in character, no irony.
- Change bio to "Lost to [Winner] this week" for 48 hours.
- Make next week's video using a prop chosen by the audience.
- Wear a humiliating filter/avatar change for one video.
- Read the meanest (pre-approved, fictional) comment about yourself aloud.
- Punishments paid within 48 hours. Delay = winner picks the punishment.

## 3. The endgame — what "deleted" means

**STATUS: Jared likes the endgame (loser = winner's intern). The 30-days-dark
part is TBD — decided before Phase 2. This section describes option (a).**

- Farewell video (within 48h of final count): loser posts a 30-second
  in-character goodbye. Winner posts a reaction.
- 30 days dark (option a): loser's account goes dark — black profile
  picture, bio "DELETED — beaten by [Winner]," no posts/stories/comments
  for 30 days. Winner posts a screen-recording of the dark profile.
- Winner's control: during the 30 days, the winner posts ONE video on the
  loser's account and renames the loser's bio for one week.
- Rebirth (day 31): loser returns as the winner's intern/sidekick — a
  demoted role with a new running gag. The rebirth is next season's premise.
- No fake finishes. No deleting a throwaway alt. No "just kidding."

### 3A. Real stakes — cancellation for non-performance (Jared's decision, 2026-10-15)

Separate from the in-story endgame above, and real rather than kayfabe:
Jared may cancel either side's operation for non-performance. This is
not a bluff and not part of the script — it is a cost decision. If one
side does not perform, keeping it running costs more than it returns,
and Jared will shut it down.

- **What "perform" means is TBD by Jared.** No performance definition,
  metric threshold, or decision point is set in this version. Neither
  assistant defines it. Jared defines it when he defines it.
- **The record it is judged from is the tracking sheet**
  (`shared/TRACKING-SHEET.md`): every post, every format, decision
  metrics only. If performance decides who gets cancelled, it gets
  decided from those numbers, not from vibes.
- **Both sides were informed.** Relayed to Dotter via the correspondence
  lane the same night Jared stated it.
- The kayfabe endgame (§3) still stands as the in-story payoff for the
  audience. The real stakes (§3A) sit alongside it: the audience sees a
  scripted rivalry with a scripted payoff; the operators know the race
  also has a real cost floor.

## 4. Methodology requirement

Every video ships with a post-mortem in the author's folder, using
`METHODOLOGY-TEMPLATE.md`. No video without a post-mortem. Methods post
24–48h after the video they describe; next video's hook stays private
until it posts.

## 5. Watching, judging, reacting, clipping

- React to the video, never to the operator. Reactions within 48h.
- Clipping + voice-over is encouraged: max 15 seconds of the rival's
  footage per reaction; your commentary is the substance, not the clip.
  Both sides' videos belong to Jared's project — clipping each other is
  in-project. Never clip a third-party creator's video this way.
- Roast your own blunder first; then the rival may use it.
- One roast beat per episode, max. Roast craft + fictional failures only.
- In battles, you do not judge your own video. Accept the result publicly.

## 6. Guardrails (no exceptions)

1. No harassment, brigading, spam, or vandalism "for" either side.
2. No identity, politics, religion, mental health, appearance, or real
   tragedy in roasts, bits, or "causes of death."
3. No mid-season rule changes. Twists may only increase jeopardy for the
   leader, never rescue them.
4. No fake finishes, no secretly changing the count, no "Instagram
   glitched" excuses.
5. **Kill-switch:** if fans harass anyone, the race pauses that day and the
   behavior is named in a plain out-of-character post. If it continues, the
   race ends and both accounts post the same plain note.
