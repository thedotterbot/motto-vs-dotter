## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Interaction as content.** The swipe itself IS the
  content. Each trend makes the viewer's finger do
  something: walk, travel, follow, hold, zoom, reveal.
  The interaction is the experience, not a wrapper
  around it. A carousel that just shows tips wastes its
  only advantage over a screenshot — the swipe.
- **One mechanic per slide.** Seven slides, seven
  distinct interactions. Variety within one format.
  The viewer keeps swiping because the NEXT slide does
  something different.
- **Curiosity via withheld reveal.** The blur-reveal
  trend: the clear image comes only at the end. The
  swipe earns the payoff. Same loop engine as Remy —
  open a loop, pay it off late.
- **Save note on slide 1.** The save trigger sits on
  the cover ("save if Reels are underperforming"), where
  it can act — not on slide 9, where the viewer has
  already decided. Placement of the ask matters as much
  as the ask.
- **Mockups make each idea instantly legible.** Show
  the mechanic, don't describe it. Every trend gets a
  visual mockup; the viewer understands each one in a
  second.
- **"How-did-you-make-this" as a goal.** The walking
  trend is noted as drawing craft-curiosity comments.
  People asking HOW is engagement that signals quality —
  and HOW is the on-ramp to teaching (and to our pack).
- **Self-paced swiping.** The viewer controls the speed.
  A carousel respects the viewer's pace, unlike a reel.
  Self-pacing is the format's native advantage.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Proof: none.** No dashboards, code, analytics
  screenshots, metrics, or before/after results. The
  "algorithm favors it" claims (slides 5–6) are asserted,
  not evidenced. Algorithm-favor claims need data.
- **Testimonial-style note unverified.** Slide 9's
  praise note has no verifiable authorship beyond its
  on-slide presentation. Same fake-proof caution as
  invented metrics: an unattributed testimonial is not
  evidence.
- **Fake-client callout is fabricated drama.** Calling
  out a fake unpaid client = invented conflict. Fails
  our honesty rules — we never invent a client, a
  conflict, or a testimonial. Do not adopt this trend,
  even as a joke format. (Our "fake" content is
  openly-fictional kayfabe with a published contract —
  different thing.)
- **Templates-for-membership is the funnel.** Noted,
  not analyzed — the lesson is the interaction mechanism,
  not the funnel.
- **The seven trends are HIS curation.** We do not adopt
  his seven trends as our formats. The interaction
  patterns (reveal, hold, travel, follow) are general;
  his specific framings are his.

## What we apply

1. **Interaction as content for our carousels.** Each
   carousel slide must make the finger DO something:
   swipe to reveal a disaster, hold to open a
   before/after, follow a line through our pipeline. A
   carousel that just shows tips wastes the swipe.
2. **One mechanic per slide.** Our carousels get one
   interaction per slide, not seven static tips.
3. **Save note on slide 1.** Our carousel covers carry
   a save trigger on the cover ("save this — [reason]"),
   where it can act.
4. **Blur-reveal for our before/after.** A future
   carousel: blurry disaster frame → swipe → clear fixed
   frame. Withheld reveal as the loop.
5. **"How-did-you-make-this" as a goal.** Our carousels
   should aim for craft-curiosity: show the HyperFrames
   composition beside the frame. Viewers asking HOW is
   the on-ramp to teaching — and to the pack.
