## What it is

Opening title card ("This Is How To Write Killer Text
Hooks") paired with a phone-mockup example reel and a
small presenter inset. Premise beat: the written
on-screen text matters more than what is spoken,
presented as a rapid-fire guide. Framework beat: a
diagram introduces six hook types as branching columns.
Category walkthroughs: the presenter explains categories
one by one, each paired with a concrete example mockup in
the upper panel (captured: a relatable-problem type, a
desired-result teaser type, a future-change type; other
branches shown in the diagram but not detailed in
sampling). Throughout, the presenter stays in the lower
inset, gesturing and talking to camera, while the upper
panel swaps examples and diagrams.

Note on scope: this entry studies the MECHANISM (inset
grammar, map-then-detail, example-per-category). We do
NOT adopt his six-type taxonomy — our hook craft comes
from our own research and panel, and a six-type menu
would become a template.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Text matters more than speech.** The premise is that
  the written on-screen text carries the reel, not the
  spoken words. For a muted feed, that is exactly right —
  captions are not a transcript, they are the message
  layer. Our captions philosophy already agrees; this
  entry validates it from the outside.
- **Split-screen inset grammar.** The presenter stays
  visible in a lower inset while the upper panel swaps
  examples and diagrams. Speaker + evidence
  simultaneously — no cutaway blackout, no "now look at
  this" break. The viewer never loses the human while
  the evidence changes.
- **Example per category.** Every abstract type gets a
  concrete mockup headline. Same "one proof per claim"
  as the opusjake entry. Abstraction without an example
  is a lecture; with one, it is a lesson.
- **Map-then-detail.** The branching diagram shows the
  whole taxonomy first, then the walkthrough zooms into
  branches one by one. The viewer always knows where
  they are in the map — same persistent-anchor mechanism
  as the jen_gottlieb whiteboard.
- **Specific headlines over abstract advice.** The
  examples are concrete headlines, not definitions. The
  viewer leaves with lines they can picture using.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Proof: none.** Mockup reels, screenshots of hook
  text, and a category diagram — no analytics, no
  before/after metrics, no testimonials. The framework
  is asserted, not evidenced. Illustrative mockups are
  not proof that the hooks work.
- **The six-type taxonomy is HIS framework.** We do not
  adopt it. (Also incomplete in observation: only three
  categories were captured in detail; the other branches
  were shown in the diagram only. Do not list six types
  as if all were observed.)
- **Talking-head inset delivery is not our format.** Our
  bet is character-led. The inset grammar transfers;
  the human presenter does not.
- **Superlative promise.** "Killer" hooks is hype
  without evidence — our no-slop rules ban superlative
  claims the work does not back up. Our hook claims
  must be earned by the reel that follows them.
- **Sampled frames only.** Beats outside sampling not
  verified; absence of proof beyond sampled frames is
  not absolute.

## What we apply

1. **Text-matters-more-than-speech as a stated rule.**
   Our captions are the message layer, not a transcript.
   Panel Eye check: does this beat read with sound off?
   If not, the text layer is failing.
2. **Inset grammar, puppet version.** Motto in a lower
   inset/corner while the upper panel shows the
   example/evidence — Motto reacting while footage plays.
   Use for future deconstruction reels (Motto reacts to
   his own old footage while text names the lesson).
3. **Map-then-detail.** When presenting a set (disaster
   list, pipeline stages), show the whole map first,
   then zoom into branches. Viewer always knows where
   they are.
4. **Example per category.** Every abstract claim in a
   teaching reel gets one concrete mockup or frame. No
   abstraction without an example.
