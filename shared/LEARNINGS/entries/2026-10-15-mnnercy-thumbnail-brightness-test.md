## What it is

No on-camera presenter. Animated text, thumbnails, and
charts. Beats: opening A/B comparison — same thumbnail
layout and wording, one with a red highlight, one with a
yellow highlight, marked as a 7x difference → brightness
explanation: a bar chart comparing how bright colors look
on screen (white highest, yellow close behind, red and
blue lower) → same-word demo, blue version (low
brightness) → same-word demo, yellow version (looks
brighter) → creator-pattern example: stacked thumbnails
using yellow highlights, paired with contrasting view
counts (about 135K vs. about 898K) → end card: a "never
unsee it" style line plus a follow prompt and a profile
mockup.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **The viewer runs the test on themselves.** The eye
  test is not described — it is experienced. You read
  one highlight first. That is the strongest proof form
  in this whole set: self-administered demonstration.
  Being told "yellow is brighter" is a claim; noticing
  you read the yellow one first is evidence you generated.
- **Hold everything constant.** Same layout, same
  wording, only the highlight color changes. The
  cleanest possible comparison — and the reel shows its
  method. One variable, everything else fixed. That is
  why the demo is convincing even without a dashboard.
- **Mechanism after result.** First the eye test (the
  WHAT), then the brightness chart (the WHY). Result
  first, explanation second. Reversed order would be a
  lecture; this order is a reveal.
- **"Never unsee it" as a button.** The closing frames
  the reel as a permanent perception shift: you leave
  seeing thumbnails differently. A viewer who feels
  changed sends the reel ("you'll never unsee this").
  The button is not "follow me" — it is "you are now
  different."
- **Concrete numbers throughout.** Percentages,
  view-count contrasts, a 7x badge. Numbers give the
  eye test a scoreboard.
- **No presenter needed.** Graphics carry it. Proof
  that a reel can be fully graphic-led when the demo is
  self-running.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **The 7x claim is not a controlled test.** The 135K
  vs. 898K counts are two different thumbnails on (presumably)
  different videos — different audiences, timing, topics.
  Confounded. The eye test itself is real (you run it);
  the 7x attribution to color alone is not proven by
  those counts. Do not copy the move of attaching a big
  multiplier to an uncontrolled comparison.
- **Brightness percentages unsourced.** The chart is
  shown without a source. Treat it as illustrative
  unless verified — a chart with no source is set
  dressing, not data.
- **Generic end-card CTA.** The follow prompt is the
  weakest beat — standard. The "never unsee it" line is
  the real button; the follow ask rides on it.
- **"AI content" label noted.** The post carries the
  platform's AI-content label. Our content is openly AI
  too — consistent with our decision to toggle the
  AI-generated-profile label on.

## What we apply

1. **Self-administered demo as a reel device.** A future
   reel where the viewer experiences the claim instead
   of being told it: two captions, spot the lie; two
   frames, which reads first. If the viewer generates
   the evidence, the reel does not need to argue.
2. **Hold one variable constant.** When we compare two
   versions (caption A vs. B, cover A vs. B), change ONE
   variable and show both, everything else identical.
   Show the method, not just the result.
3. **"Never unsee it" button.** A future reel ends with
   a perception shift the viewer carries out of the reel
   — a framing they cannot unsee. Panel Comic check: does
   this reel change how the viewer sees something after
   it ends?
4. **Mechanism after result.** Show the WHAT first (the
   demo), the WHY second (the explanation). Never lecture
   first.
5. **Color-brightness for our own covers.** Yellow reads
   brighter than red/blue on screen. Test a yellow
   highlight for the key word on reel covers and title
   cards (currently teal/cream). One variable, measure
   the difference honestly.
