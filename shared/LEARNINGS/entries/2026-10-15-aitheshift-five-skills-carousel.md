## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Carousel as saveable reference.** A numbered
  checklist you save and swipe back through. Carousels
  earn saves — the exact metric our plan wants from our
  ~1x biweekly carousel slot. The format IS the save
  trigger: a reference you keep beats a performance you
  watch once.
- **Numbered skills, countable.** 1–5, large numbered
  labels. Same mnemonic mechanism as the jen_gottlieb
  entry: countability makes it memorable and re-findable
  ("skill 3 was the references one").
- **Recurring mascot on every slide.** The same 3D
  character appears throughout. Character consistency
  makes a static format feel like a series, not a deck.
  The mascot is the thread through the slides.
- **Dense collages: tool + input + output.** Each slide
  mixes UI screenshots, code snippets, and example
  outputs. One slide shows the tool, the input, and the
  result together — same cause-and-effect density as the
  split-screen demo in the piyush.glitch entry.
- **Concrete named tools.** Not abstract advice. Names
  are checkable; abstractions are not.
- **High-contrast design.** Black/yellow, large labels.
  Readable at thumbnail size, distinct at a glance.
- **Cover promises three things in one line.** A number
  (5), free, and an outcome (higher-quality animation).
  Three promises, one line — the reader knows exactly
  what the swipe buys them.
- **Outro gesture as CTA.** The character reaches toward
  the viewer. A gesture does the asking — character as
  call to action, not a text button.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Proof: none.** No metrics, no before/after, no
  testimonials. Screenshots are illustrative; the small
  code text was not even legible at browser zoom. A
  checklist of tools is not evidence the tools deliver.
- **The five skills are HIS curation.** We do not adopt
  his list. Our stack is our own (HyperFrames + GSAP +
  QA + gates). Overlap: our pipeline already uses GSAP
  timelines and Lottie files (Sprint 4) — that overlap
  validates that those tools are standard for this work;
  it is not his influence.
- **"PRO-LEVEL" is a superlative without evidence.**
  Our no-slop rules ban superlative claims the work does
  not back up. Our carousel covers promise what the
  slides deliver — no "pro-level" inflation.
- **"Free skills" needs the paid-runtime disclosure.**
  The skills may be free; Claude Code is paid. Same
  disclosure our pack needs: install is free, the
  runtime is paid. Never "free" without the asterisk.
- **Follow-beg outro is the weakest slide.** A generic
  follow prompt. Our carousels should end with a
  save-trigger (a checklist recap), not a follow beg.
