## What it is

A hype-driven side-hustle reel: reaction-style opening on a
borrowed podcast clip of a famous creator, then B-roll, talking
head, and a phone-prop close. The pitch is a money claim built
from big numbers and a low-effort promise.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Pattern-interrupt hook.** First frame: a famous face
  (borrowed podcast clip) + an absurd future-year claim + a
  big dollar figure + the creator's small reaction window
  bottom-left. Four attention grabs in one frame, before a
  word lands. The viewer stops for the celebrity; stays for
  the absurd claim.
- **Specificity as credibility.** Concrete dollar figure,
  future-year claim, small daily time commitment, a named
  tool, a year reference. Every abstract claim gets a number
  attached. This is the specificity principle working exactly
  as written — and exactly why it is dangerous without proof.
- **No static frames.** Very fast cuts: podcast clip →
  desk B-roll → talking head → phone prop, a new shot every
  few seconds. Bold white captions with black outline, lower
  third, changing every beat, readable with sound off.
- **Phone prop close.** Holding up a phone showing the
  product name makes an abstract offer feel like a physical
  thing. Cheap, effective, stealable staging.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Proof: none.** No earnings dashboard, bank statement,
  analytics screen, contract, or documentary evidence of any
  kind in the observed frames. Only text overlays and a phone
  screen showing a product name. The reel works on claim
  density, not evidence. We do not do this. Our rule stands:
  show process that produces the outcome shown, or show the
  disaster honestly. A claim without proof is a fail for us,
  however well it performs for him.
- **Borrowed celebrity clip as the hook.** The opening's
  stopping power is rented from someone else's fame. We don't
  have a famous face to borrow, and building a hook that
  depends on one is not a repeatable skill.
- **Hype delivery.** Fast, urgent, high-energy direct address.
  It fits a money-claim reel. It would read as false coming
  from a plush learning to make videos. Our delivery is
  bewildered and self-aware, not urgent.

## What we apply

1. **Stack the first frame.** Four grabs in one frame (face +
   absurd claim + number + reaction window) is the standard
   our hook frame should be judged against. Our hook chip +
   Motto on screen + a concrete number is the character-content
   version.
2. **Phone-prop thinking.** A physical object that makes an
   abstract thing concrete. Our equivalent: a prop Motto holds
   or points at (a tag, a chip, a printed number) instead of a
   caption floating in space.
3. **Caption discipline.** Bold, outlined, lower-third,
   changing every beat, readable muted. Our kinetic captions
   should be checked against this: if a beat's caption isn't
   readable with sound off, it fails.
