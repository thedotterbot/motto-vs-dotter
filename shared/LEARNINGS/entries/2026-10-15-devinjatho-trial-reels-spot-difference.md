## What it is

Close-up direct-to-camera setup with a large "?" overlay
and a 5.6M-view clip behind the speaker — a
spot-the-difference curiosity setup. Then: screen
recording of an Instagram Trial Reels grid showing
multiple versions of the same clip with different text
hooks and a 24:00:00 timer → speaker to camera with a
"LAST MONTH" overlay, background showing his
profile/dashboard with 42.8M accounts reached and 486K
followers → speaker gesturing, search field showing "Tr"
being typed → split-screen comparison of the same clip at
44.5K views versus 5.6M views, with a banner stating the
same video got 125X views, then speaker continuing below
the comparison.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Spot-the-difference as a hook.** A "?" overlay plus
  two versions. The viewer plays a game instead of
  receiving a lesson. Curiosity gap: what changed
  between the two versions? The question carries the
  watch.
- **Same-clip comparison.** Same video, different text
  hooks. Hold-everything-constant method (same as the
  mnnercy entry): one variable changed, everything else
  fixed. The cleanest possible comparison — and the
  reel shows both versions side by side.
- **The tool interface on screen.** The Trial Reels grid
  itself is shown — multiple versions, timer visible.
  The viewer sees the mechanism, not just hears about
  it. Showing the interface makes the tactic feel real
  and usable.
- **24:00:00 timer as a concrete process detail.** A
  time bound makes a system feel real. "Test it" is
  vague; "24 hours, then read it" is a procedure.
- **Dramatic contrast numbers.** 44.5K vs. 5.6M. The
  gap IS the hook. Same mechanism as the thumbnail
  entry: a big visible gap earns the watch.
- **Time-bounded claim + account-level numbers.**
  "LAST MONTH" plus 42.8M reached scopes the claim —
  not one cherry-picked video, a month of account
  performance. Scope makes a claim harder to dismiss.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **125X is confounded.** Same video, different hooks —
  but also different distribution (Trial Reels shows to
  non-followers), different timing, algorithm variance.
  The hook is not proven to be the sole cause of the
  gap. A 125X banner attributes everything to one
  variable; the test does not isolate it.
- **Numbers are as-displayed graphics.** No analytics
  dashboard shown, no independent verification. The
  view counts are graphics in the video, not evidence
  from an analytics screen. Same proof gap as most of
  this set.
- **His scale does not validate the tactic for us.**
  42.8M reached / 486K followers are HIS account's
  numbers. Our account is at ~1 follower. A tactic that
  works at 486K may behave differently at zero — the
  entry is a mechanism to bank, not a result to expect.
- **Trial Reels unavailable to us until 1K followers.**
  Standing rule: the toggle does not appear until a
  public account hits 1,000 followers. We cannot use
  this tactic yet. Bank the mechanism for later.
- **Talking-head delivery is not our format.** Our bet
  is character-led. The explainer energy works here; it
  does not transfer to a plush puppet's delivery.

## What we apply

1. **Spot-the-difference as a reel device.** A future
   reel shows two versions of the same frame/clip and
   asks the viewer to spot what changed (hook, caption,
   framing). Curiosity gap + self-administered test.
2. **Hold one variable.** When we test hooks or covers,
   change ONE variable (same clip, different hook) and
   show both versions side by side. Show the method,
   not just the result.
3. **Trial Reels plan confirmed.** At 1K followers,
   trials become the default for uncertain posts: same
   clip, different hooks, 24h read, share the winner
   manually. Auto-share stays OFF. This entry validates
   the mechanism — it does not change the plan.
4. **Timer as a process detail.** When we show a system,
   include a concrete time bound (24h) so it feels like
   a procedure, not a vibe.
5. **Same-clip comparison for our own hook tests.** Reel
   02's hook vs. an alternate hook, same footage, shown
   split-screen. Our version of his 44.5K vs. 5.6M beat.
