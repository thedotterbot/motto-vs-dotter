## What it is

Opening: blunt, repeated-posting instruction delivered
straight to camera — post everything five times. Premise:
the same reel should be posted again in different
placements/formats rather than made fresh each time.
On-screen list builds step by step: trial version,
main-feed version, then mirrored versions of each. Payoff
claim: reposting this way gains more algorithmic reach,
engagement, and followers. Close: a reminder not to
forget the strategy and to post the reel again in another
form. Single static kitchen setting; large bold
word-by-word captions with key words highlighted.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Audacity as hook.** "Post it five times" is a
  memorable, quotable, slightly outrageous prescription.
  The audacity IS the hook — it sounds extreme enough to
  stop the scroll, and the self-aware delivery signals
  he knows it.
- **Accumulating list.** The on-screen list builds step
  by step as he speaks — trial, main-feed, mirrored
  versions. The viewer watches the system assemble
  instead of being handed a finished list. Assembly is
  more memorable than display.
- **Concrete variants, not vague advice.** Named
  placements (trial, main-feed, mirrored), not
  "repurpose more." A viewer leaves knowing exactly
  what to post where.
- **Single static setting.** One kitchen, no scene
  changes. Focus stays on speaker + text. Same
  constraint-as-focus as the jun_yuh split-screen entry.
- **Caption emphasis as a second channel.** Word-by-word
  captions with key words highlighted. The highlight
  tells the muted viewer what matters without them
  having to read everything.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Proof: none.** No dashboards, code, screenshots,
  metrics, before/after, or testimonials. The payoff
  claim (more reach, engagement, followers) is asserted,
  not shown. "Five times" is a prescription without
  evidence — no data that five beats two or three. The
  number may be rhetorical, not tested.
- **Reposting identical content risks fatigue.** The
  reel addresses algorithmic gain only, never audience
  cost. Followers seeing the same reel five times may
  mute or unfollow. A tactic that gains reach while
  losing followers is a bad trade — and this reel never
  prices it.
- **Mirrored versions are near-duplicates.** Flipping
  is a cosmetic change. Whether the algorithm treats
  flipped versions as fresh content is unverified in
  this reel. A variant the viewer cannot distinguish
  from the original is duplication with extra steps.
- **Assumes volume we don't have.** At 3 new reels per
  week, "post everything five times" would mean 15
  posts a week of the same 3 reels. Premature for us —
  new work is the engine at our stage, not reposting.
- **Talking-head delivery is not our format.** Our bet
  is character-led. The explainer energy works here; it
  does not transfer to a plush puppet's delivery.

## What we apply

1. **Repurpose, don't duplicate.** Each reel gets
   planned variants: trial version (at 1K), main-feed
   version, and ONE genuinely different variant (new
   hook, new caption, or new framing — a difference the
   viewer notices). Flipped-alone is cosmetic, not a
   variant.
2. **Accumulating list as a device.** When presenting a
   system, build the on-screen list step by step rather
   than showing it all at once. Assembly beats display.
3. **Audacity as hook test.** A future hook can be a
   blunt prescription. Panel Comic check: is the
   extreme claim one we can back with evidence? If not,
   it stays a joke, not a promise.
4. **Variants ride on top; new work is the engine.**
   Cadence stays 3/week NEW reels. Variants (trial +
   main) are leverage on top of new work — they never
   replace it.
