## What it is

A numbered countdown, 1 to 10, of viral video formats. Same
template every beat: yellow title top, numbered format label
bottom-center, creator centered gesturing, and a small
vertical inset clip showing an example of that format.
Each beat lasts ~1–2 seconds. Then it loops back to 1.

The 10 formats (from on-screen labels + caption): duplicate
self / two versions of one person; side-by-side comparison;
person speaking with a graphic behind them; narration over
already-filmed clips; numbered list; worst-to-best ranking;
reacting to another person's content; showing on-screen
steps / tutorial; casual unscripted storytime; direct-to-
camera single-idea talking head.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Countable + template-consistent = completion loop.**
  The viewer knows exactly what they're getting (10 items)
  and exactly how it will arrive (same template each time).
  The only variable is the next example. That is a
  completion engine: stay to finish the list.
- **Saveable reference as the share trigger.** The video's
  value is being a checklist. People save checklists and
  send them to creator friends ("share this with a creator
  who's stuck on ideas"). The save trigger is the content,
  not a CTA.
- **One new example per beat.** Every 1–2 seconds, a new
  inset clip. Rapid visual variety with zero production
  cost — the examples are other people's clips. The
  presenter's energy + the inset swap carry all the motion.
- **No word-by-word subtitles — and it works.** Only large
  bold yellow graphic text: title top, numbered label
  bottom. When the structure is this legible, subtitles
  are redundant. The number IS the caption.
- **Promise specificity.** "10 formats" is concrete and
  countable. A vague "formats that go viral" would not
  hold attention the same way. The number makes the
  promise checkable.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Proof: none.** No analytics, view counts, follower
  counts, or before/after metrics inside the video. The
  inset clips illustrate each format; they don't evidence
  that the formats work. A reference list gets away with
  this because its value is the list itself. Our comedy
  reels can't — a joke with no payoff has no value.
- **Borrowed examples.** The inset clips are other
  creators' work, used as illustrations. We can't fill our
  reels with other people's clips and call it ours. Our
  "examples" have to be our own footage (our disasters).
- **Listicle pace vs joke pace.** 1–2 seconds per beat
  works for a reference list. It would murder a joke,
  which needs setup + payoff timing. Don't import the
  tempo; import the structure.

## What we apply

1. **Saveable-reference reel as a format.** A countable,
   template-consistent reel from OUR knowledge (e.g. our
   10 biggest disasters, ranked worst-to-best — format #6
   on his list) is a legitimate save-trigger format for us.
   One new example per beat, same template, no filler.
2. **Format #1 (duplicate self) = our debate-duel.** Two
   versions of one person in one clip. Already logged from
   katecore — this entry independently confirms it as a
   named viral format.
3. **Format #6 (worst-to-best ranking) as a reel shape.**
   Rank our disasters worst-to-best with a visual tier.
   The ranking IS the retention: viewers stay to see #1.
4. **Number as caption.** When a reel is countable, the
   number on screen replaces subtitles. Use for list/rank
   reels: big number, short label, no sentence needed.
