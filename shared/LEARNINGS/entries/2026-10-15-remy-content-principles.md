## What it is

A ~1,850-word Claude skill: 10 principles about viewer psychology
for short-form scripts. Its central move is an instruction to
apply the principles silently — never repeat the headings, never
turn them into a checklist or output format. Most content skills
hand the model a format, and every output sounds the same. This
one tries to force reasoning from scratch each time.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Loops, not claims.** The skill's core is that a video runs
  on open questions the viewer holds, paid one at a time, with a
  fresh loop opening around two-thirds through. Our reels
  explain; they don't loop. That is a real gap in our scripts.
- **Value delivered early.** Promise in the first seconds,
  DELIVER something genuinely useful or surprising by ~5–10
  seconds. Our theme bit is an explanation block before the
  joke — it fails this test as written.
- **Objection preempts.** Every video creates its own reason to
  scroll ("this is an ad," "won't work for me"). The skill says:
  know which one THIS video provokes, disarm it in one line
  where the doubt lands. We never do this.
- **Specificity in the object.** Concrete numbers, names, and
  dates in the thing being described — never in naming the
  audience. Naming the viewer's job excludes more than it hooks.
- **Line-by-line.** Each line must sell the next one. Our reels
  had dead stretches where no line was selling anything.
- **Proof carries the pitch.** The reel itself leans on metrics
  screenshots + a framework diagram, not claims. Same lesson
  as our nothing beat: show evidence, don't assert progress.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- Built for talking-head educational content. Zero comedy
  craft: nothing on jokes, punch words, heightening, character,
  or performance. The loop/pacing principles transfer; the
  comedy does not exist here.
- The "bigger number" instinct (six beats three) is a listicle
  reflex that fights one-premise comedy. For jokes, one premise
  beats six.
- No distribution mechanics, no production constraints, no
  worked examples. "500 hours" is a claim, not evidence — the
  skill contains no case studies or before/after scripts.
- His format needs no orientation (his face is the
  orientation). Character content pays an orientation cost he
  never faces. Do not delete our theme bit on his say-so —
  test it.

## What we apply

1. Panel additions (Comic / Stranger hats): loop check (open
   question at every beat? fresh loop ~65%?), line-sells-next-
   line check, objection-preempt check (which objection does
   this reel provoke? disarmed in one line?).
2. Theme-bit audit against the fluff rule: does it serve the
   hook's promise, or is it dead air? Compress or fold
   orientation INTO the joke — test, don't assume.
3. Pack differentiation: his skill is principles-only. Our
   pack's edge is principles + worked examples from real
   production + the production pipeline (voice, QA, gates).
   Never his framework, phrasing, or examples.
