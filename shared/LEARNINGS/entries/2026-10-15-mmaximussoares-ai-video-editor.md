## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Hand-on-laptop makes the tool feel tangible.** A
  hand on a keyboard = someone actually using it. That
  beats a floating mockup: tangibility over polish.
  The close hand shots are the most convincing frames in
  the reel.
- **Talking-head ↔ demo alternation.** Two-register
  pacing — person, then evidence, then person. Same
  three-mode variety as the last several entries; the
  alternation itself is the pacing.
- **Label every demo.** A title overlay on the demo
  names what you are looking at. The viewer never
  wonders "what is this screen?" Labeling is a small
  courtesy with a large comprehension payoff.
- **Specificity in the promise.** "Edit like favorite
  creators / break down a creator's style" is a concrete
  job, not generic "AI editing." A promise with a
  concrete job is checkable; a vague promise is not.
- **Setting with personality.** Warm indoor room with
  posters. The room is a character detail, not a blank
  backdrop. A set with details beats a void.
- **Large high-contrast word-by-word captions.**
  Muted-readable, same caption discipline as the strong
  entries.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Proof: none.** No dashboard, code, metrics,
  before/after, or testimonials. The demo footage is
  stylized/illustrative editing UI — and whether it is
  the real product or a mockup could not be verified.
  Same mock-UI caution as the ai.nxtlvl entry: a mockup
  presented as product misleads.
- **"Edit like favorite creators" is a style-mimicry
  promise with no line drawn.** Breaking down a
  creator's style to learn pacing and cuts = studying
  mechanism. Replicating their voice, jokes, or identity
  = copying material. The reel does not draw the line,
  and we must. Our learnings rule draws it: steal the
  mechanism, never the material.
- **Talking-head delivery is not our format.** Our bet
  is character-led. The explainer energy works here; it
  does not transfer to a plush puppet's delivery.
- **No joke observed.** Shareability rides on
  curiosity/promise and fast pacing, not comedy. Our
  reels need the joke too.

## What we apply

1. **Hand-on-keyboard tangibility.** When we demo our
   pipeline, show hands-on-keyboard / cursor-in-
   composition shots — real screen recording of our
   real composition — so the tool feels used, not
   rendered.
2. **Label every demo.** Title overlay on any screen
   demo naming what it is (our HyperFrames composition,
   our QA report). Viewer never wonders what the screen
   shows.
3. **Style-breakdown line, drawn explicitly.** When we
   study a creator's style, name what we take (structure,
   pacing, caption rhythm = mechanism) and what we never
   take (jokes, voice, identity = material). The line
   gets stated, not assumed.
4. **Setting with personality.** Our set (wall, floor,
   desk, bulb from reel 03) keeps its details. A set
   with personality beats a blank backdrop.
5. **Alternation grammar.** Talking-head ↔ demo is a
   pacing template. Ours is Motto ↔ evidence: puppet,
   then proof, then puppet.
