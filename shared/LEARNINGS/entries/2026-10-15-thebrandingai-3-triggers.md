## What it is

A voiceover-driven deconstruction. It opens on a viral
example clip (a man eating at a fast-food table) framed as
a high-view success case, then cycles through three
attention mechanisms: each one gets a black screen with
large kinetic typography, one word or phrase at a time,
interleaved with short example clips illustrating it.
No talking head, no presenter, no character. Text is the
primary carrier, not a supplement. Closes with a brief
funnel/CTA beat (not analyzed).

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Deconstruction is the format.** Take a familiar viral
  clip, reveal the hidden mechanics behind it, package
  them as a small numbered set. The viewer gets two
  pleasures: recognizing the clip, then feeling smarter
  about why it worked. That is a saveable structure.
- **Specificity in the hook.** The opening carries an
  exact view-count claim and a numbered framework (3
  triggers). A number plus a count is a promise the
  viewer can check off: three items, then done.
- **Text as the carrier.** Huge bold all-caps kinetic
  typography on black, words appearing one at a time.
  No conventional subtitles. The text IS the content;
  the clips illustrate it. In a muted feed, this reel
  still reads.
- **A visual change every 1–2 seconds.** Rapid cycling
  between full-screen text cards and short example
  clips. The alternation itself holds attention — same
  cut-alternation rhythm as the brainrot entry, applied
  to an explainer instead of a gag.
- **Example footage makes abstract concrete.** Each
  mechanism gets a clip (restaurant awkwardness,
  talent-show reaction, sitcom meme). The viewer never
  has to imagine what the tactic looks like; they see
  it. Analogy by footage, not by description.
- **Inset-box technique.** One example clip plays in a
  small box at the top while large text fills the lower
  screen. Two information channels at once without
  clutter.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Repurposed footage.** The example clips are other
  people's material (restaurant shoot, TV talent-show
  clip, sitcom meme). We do not build on repurposed
  footage — that step breaks platform terms and
  copyright, and repurposed content is barred from
  monetization. Our deconstruction material has to be
  our own footage.
- **Proof: none.** The big view-count claim appears
  only as overlaid text on the example clip. No
  analytics dashboard, no view counter, no screenshot
  of metrics. A number in big type is not evidence.
  Do not copy the move of stating a metric without
  showing it.
- **No character, no presenter.** This reel has no
  host at all — voiceover plus text cards. That is the
  opposite of our bet (Motto as the recognizable face).
  The deconstruction format works here; the faceless
  delivery does not transfer to a character channel.
- **Numbered-framework risk.** "3 triggers" is clean,
  but it is also the most templated shape on the feed.
  Our scripts rotate shapes for a reason. A numbered
  explainer is fine occasionally; as a default it reads
  generic.

## What we apply

1. **Deconstruct our own failures.** A future reel
   dissects one of OUR reels (reel 01 or 02) with
   numbered mechanics: what failed, what the fix was,
   shown with our own footage. Same deconstruction
   engine, original material, real proof (our frames,
   our numbers).
2. **One-word-at-a-time text cards as a caption style
   test.** For a future explainer beat, try black cards
   with one phrase at a time instead of kinetic captions
   over footage. Animatic check: does it read muted?
3. **Hook with a verified number.** When a reel opens
   with a number, it must be one we can show: our real
   disaster count, our real follower count, our real
   engagement. Never an overlay-only claim.
4. **Inset-box grammar.** Small clip on top, big text
   below — usable for a future beat where Motto reacts
   to his own old footage while text names the lesson.
