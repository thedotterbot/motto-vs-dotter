## What it is

Title-card intro claiming a top AI skill, with a row of
well-known brand logos underneath. Talking-head opening
in a kitchen: credibility setup about prior work running
ads at Meta (first caption words observed: "I used").
Screen recording of a dark AI chat/tool interface scanning
large volumes of comments. Talking-head explanation: turn
real customer language — problems, desires — into testable
ad angles. Mid-video overlay referencing building two
8-figure businesses, with small inset clips. Overlay card
listing three prioritized angles to test first. Full-screen
table screenshot ranking founder statements by evidence
strength, with columns for themes, comment counts, and
strength ratings. Video loops; no distinct final CTA frame
was isolated in sampling.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Credibility first.** Past work running ads at Meta,
  stated up front. The viewer grants attention because
  the speaker has done the job. Credibility before
  content — the hook is the résumé, not the promise.
- **Real language as the raw material.** Turn customer
  comments into angles. The insight: the audience writes
  your copy for you. Evidence-based creativity — angles
  mined from real words beat angles invented in a
  brainstorm.
- **Ranked table as the takeaway.** Founder statements
  ranked by evidence strength, with comment counts and
  strength ratings. A prioritized list, not a pile of
  ideas. Ranking IS the value — anyone can list ideas;
  ranking them by evidence is the work.
- **Three prioritized first-tests.** The viewer leaves
  with "test these three first," not "test everything."
  A countable, ordered next step beats a vague
  encouragement.
- **Kitchen setting vs. business claims.** Casual
  setting contrasts with big claims. Informality makes
  big claims feel conversational, not pitchy.
- **Process shown.** Screen recording of the tool
  scanning comments — the viewer sees the work happen.
  Process shown beats process described (same as the
  Borja entry: demo IS the content).
- **Fast word-by-word captions synced to speech.**
  Muted-readable, same caption discipline as the strong
  entries.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **8-figure claim is an overlay, not evidence.** No
  revenue dashboard, no verified metric. A claim on
  screen is not a number with a source. Same
  unverified-claim pattern as most of this set.
- **Brand logos under a claim.** A row of well-known
  logos beneath a "top skill" claim implies association
  or endorsement. We do not float other brands' logos
  as implied endorsement. Our equivalents are our own
  artifacts (real reels, real QA reports).
- **"Top AI skill" is a superlative without evidence.**
  Our no-slop rules ban superlatives the work does not
  back up.
- **The ranked table's data source is unverified.** A
  table with columns LOOKS rigorous — but rigor requires
  a verifiable source. A screenshot of a table is not
  evidence the underlying data is real. Tables need
  sources, same as charts.
- **The skill is HER product.** We do not copy her
  angles method as a product. The PRINCIPLE (mine real
  language, rank by evidence) is general and transfers
  as a mechanism.
- **Talking-head delivery is not our format.** Our bet
  is character-led. The explainer energy works here; it
  does not transfer to a plush puppet's delivery.

## What we apply

1. **Mine real language.** Our scripts and hooks should
   mine REAL viewer language — comments, DMs, our own
   post-mortem words — instead of inventing hooks from
   nothing. When we have comments, the comments write
   the next hooks.
2. **Rank by evidence.** When we present options
   (hooks, angles, ideas), rank them by evidence
   strength — our tracking-sheet data (saves / shares /
   follows per 1K) — instead of an unranked pile.
3. **Credibility first for teaching reels.** When Motto
   teaches, open with the credential that earns it: our
   real production ("I made 4 reels and killed one") —
   real, checkable, no superlatives.
4. **Three prioritized first-tests.** When a teaching
   reel gives advice, end with three prioritized first
   actions, not a vague "try this."
5. **Table as a takeaway format.** A ranked table
   (themes × counts × strength) is a saveable format
   for a future carousel — our disaster ledger as a
   ranked table.
