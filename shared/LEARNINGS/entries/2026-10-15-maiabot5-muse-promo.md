## What it is

The first of the two reference reels Jared shared at the
start of this project — the official Muse promo. A small
3D CGI furry mascot in a softly lit pink/beige studio,
cradling a glowing orb in both hands, gently bobbing and
gazing at it. Then a tilted phone mockup slides in showing
an Instagram feed: the same mascot as a post, with a Share
dialog overlaid. The reel loops between those two
compositions. No burned-in captions, no text cards, no
other scenes. No voiceover or human on camera observed
visually.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **The character IS the product.** No explanation, no
  feature list. A cute mascot holding a glowing orb tells
  you "this thing is friendly and a little magical" in one
  frame. Character design doing the job a script usually
  does.
- **The meta joke is the send trigger.** The reel depicts
  itself being shared: the mascot inside its own Instagram
  post, Share dialog open. The viewer is shown the exact
  action the reel wants. Self-reference as call to action,
  without a single spoken word asking for it.
- **One focal point.** The glowing orb. The mascot gazes
  at it, cradles it with both hands, and the viewer's eye
  goes where the mascot's goes. A prop that directs
  attention beats a prop that decorates.
- **Two compositions, total.** Mascot solo, then mascot +
  phone. That is the whole reel. Simplicity reads as
  confidence. Contrast with our instinct to pack every
  reel with beats, captions, and gags.
- **Gentle movement as tone.** Slow bobbing and floating,
  no fast dancing or exaggerated gestures. The calm
  movement matches the cute character. Movement quality
  IS character: a frantic mascot would read as a
  different product.
- **Legible with no words.** No captions, no text cards,
  no voiceover needed for the visual read. The phone
  mockup's UI text (Share, likes line) is set dressing,
  not the message.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Full 3D CGI.** Smooth fur, soft studio lighting,
  gentle continuous bobbing — this is the 3D bar our
  cutout pipeline cannot match. We can copy the staging
  (mascot + prop + phone), not the render quality.
- **Their mascot is not ours.** This is Meta's official
  Muse character. Motto is our own dusty-teal design
  with round dark glasses. Do not drift Motto toward
  their mascot (cream/beige, hooded onesie, dot eyes).
  The resemblance Jared approved is to Jolly's general
  plush language, not to this specific character.
- **Fake proof in the mockup.** The animated phone shows
  a likes count on the in-animation post. That number is
  fiction — part of the mockup, not evidence of real
  results. Do not copy the move of putting invented
  metrics inside a phone mockup. Our phone mockups show
  our real frames, never invented counts.
- **No captions is a luxury of cuteness.** This reel can
  skip captions because the mascot is adorable and the
  joke is visual. Our character is unknown and our
  premises are verbal. We cannot skip captions yet.

## What we apply

1. **Meta beat, built with our own material.** A future
   reel: Motto holds a code-built phone showing his OWN
   reel with a Share dialog over it. Same self-reference
   engine, our footage, no invented metrics.
2. **One prop as a focal point per reel.** Give Motto one
   thing to gaze at, cradle, or handle — a glowing orb
   equivalent — and let his eyes direct the viewer's
   eyes to it. Prop directs attention, not decorates.
3. **Two-composition simplicity as a test.** One future
   reel built from just two alternating framings. If the
   premise is strong, the reel does not need eight beats.
4. **Movement quality matches character.** Our idle bob
   already exists (L-19 fixed its coverage). Keep the
   bob gentle on tender beats, bigger on comic beats.
   Speed of movement is a character choice, not just
   a pacing choice.
