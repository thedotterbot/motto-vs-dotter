## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Re-cut, not duplicate.** Same premise, different
  edit, different visuals, posted a day later. The
  viewer gets a NEW video, not a rerun. This is the
  brock11johnson idea executed right: "repurpose, don't
  duplicate" in practice. A variant must be re-cut with
  new evidence — that is what separates it from a
  re-upload.
- **One-hour documentary as proof-of-concept.** A
  serious, long-form output as the proof. Bigger claim
  than a 30-second clip: if code can make an hour, it can
  make anything. Serious-output proof (same as the first
  VID reel's eruption clip, scaled up).
- **End-to-end workflow shown.** Research → script →
  code-designed scenes → generated missing shots →
  local render → tweak-by-chat. The full pipeline, not
  one trick. Same layers-as-system story as the
  bradfordmarais entry.
- **Missing-shots beat.** Naming the failure mode
  (footage that does not exist) and showing the fix
  (generate it as motion graphics). Problem → fix
  grammar. Naming the gap makes the fix believable.
- **Tweak-by-chat: iteration as a feature.** The
  workflow includes revision, not just generation. More
  honest than the first reel's clean prompt → output —
  iteration is shown as part of the method.
- **Tool-agnostic positioning.** "Works across named AI
  coding tools." The method, not the tool, is the
  product. Tool-agnostic claims age better than
  tool-specific ones.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Proof is illustrative mockups only.** Documentary
  player, script draft, code/terminal view,
  motion-graphics example — all mockups. No live
  dashboard, verified metrics, before/after, or
  testimonials. The one-hour documentary's existence is
  claimed, not verified.
- **Same premise twice in two days risks fatigue.** The
  re-cut strategy works once or twice; as a habit it
  becomes the brock11johnson trap. Variants must stay
  genuinely different — the moment a re-cut feels like a
  rerun, it costs trust.
- **The workflow is HIS framework.** We do not copy his
  seven-step pipeline. Ours is our own (script → voice →
  compose → QA → gate-8).
- **Comment-gated CTA not analyzed.** Noted, not the
  lesson.

## What we apply

1. **Re-cut, don't duplicate — with proof.** This entry
   is the evidence for the brock11johnson rule: a variant
   must be re-cut with NEW evidence (different visuals,
   different proof example), not re-uploaded. Our variant
   rule, confirmed in the wild.
2. **Serious-output proof.** When we demo the pipeline,
   use our most serious output (reel 04, the full
   pipeline product), not a toy.
3. **Missing-shots grammar.** Name the failure mode,
   then show the fix. Our post-mortems already do this;
   bring it on-screen: "the footage didn't exist → so
   we generated it."
4. **Iteration as a feature.** Our reels should show
   revision as part of the workflow (v1 → panel → v2),
   not hide it. Revision shown is credibility.
5. **End-to-end pipeline as content.** A future reel
   walks our full pipeline (script → voice → compose →
   QA → gate-8) in order, like his (a)–(f). One reel,
   the whole system.
