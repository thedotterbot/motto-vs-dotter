## What it is

The second of the two reference reels Jared shared at the
start of this project — the "new guy at the office" skit.
A small fluffy cream mascot is treated deadpan as a serious
new office hire. Beats: lounging on an orange couch under
"Be reMARKable" wall art with a "GET IT DONE." overlay;
sitting on a desk, legs dangling; standing on the desk
pointing at a dashboard graphic; walking toward the camera
through office areas while app logos float beside it
(Canva, Slack, then Notion, Dropbox, Shopify); walking
down a corridor; walking through a lobby with a "JD: GET.
THINGS. DONE." overlay — the whole skit framed as its job
description. 3D CGI mascot composited into real office
environments. No captions, no human presenter.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Incongruity is the joke.** An adorable plush-like
  creature treated deadpan as a competent new hire whose
  entire job is getting things done. Cute + competent.
  Nobody explains it; the premise is the punchline.
- **Real backgrounds make the character pop.** The
  offices, lounge, and lobby are real. A CGI character
  in a real place reads as "this thing is here" in a way
  a fully animated world does not. Contrast between real
  and rendered does staging work for free.
- **Walking toward camera = forward motion without fast
  cuts.** Each beat holds for several seconds; the camera
  is mostly static. Momentum comes from the mascot
  walking toward the viewer, not from editing. A reel
  can move without cutting.
- **The JD overlay is the button.** The closing line
  reframes the whole skit as a job description. Premise
  + button in one overlay. No outro card, no spoken CTA
  needed.
- **Logos as visual shorthand.** Floating app logos say
  "works across tools" in one frame, no explanation.
  Same mechanism as the Emergent entry (brand marks do
  labeling work captions usually do).
- **Every beat is a self-contained visual gag.** Couch,
  desk sit, dashboard point, logo walk, lobby walk —
  each one screenshots cleanly. Shareability comes from
  beats that survive as stills, not just as a sequence.
- **Two overlays carry the verbal joke.** "GET IT DONE."
  and the JD line. The rest is body language. Same lesson
  as the Muse promo: when a beat is visual, one line of
  text can carry it.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **Full 3D CGI + compositing into real offices.** Smooth
  bouncy CGI, a real waddling walk, composited into real
  environments. Our cutout pipeline cannot walk, and
  cannot composite into real footage at this quality.
  Copy the staging (mascot in a real place, walk toward
  camera), not the render.
- **True walk cycle.** The mascot does a waddling,
  forward-leaning toddle with swinging arms. Our rig
  cannot do a real walk — reel 02's bounce-shuffle
  proved it. Do NOT fake a walk again. Walking toward
  camera stays a Rive-rig target, not a cutout trick.
- **Proof: none.** The dashboard graphic (tile figures,
  bar chart) is illustrative set dressing, not a real
  screenshot or verifiable analytics. The app logos
  signal "works across tools" but demonstrate no real
  integration. Do not copy invented metrics inside a
  dashboard, and do not float other brands' logos as
  implied integrations.
- **No captions is a luxury of cuteness, again.** This
  reel skips captions because the character is adorable
  and every beat is visual. Our character is unknown
  and our premises are verbal. We cannot skip captions
  yet.

## What we apply

1. **JD-overlay button.** A future reel ends with a
   one-line "job description" overlay as the button —
   our material, our joke (e.g. a Motto job description
   that names what he actually does: disasters). Premise
   + button in one overlay, no outro card.
2. **Walk-toward-camera stays a rig target.** When the
   Rive rig exists, mascot walking toward camera through
   locations is the movement to build first. Until then,
   no fake walks. Reel 02's lesson stands.
3. **Test Motto over a real photo background.** A cheap
   version of their compositing: our sprite over a real
   office photo for one beat. If the contrast makes him
   pop, bank it as a staging option.
4. **One-line overlays for visual beats.** When a beat is
   visual, one overlay carries it — no caption stack.
   Animatic check: does this beat read with one line?
5. **Numbers must be real or clearly a joke prop.** If
   we ever show a dashboard, its figures are our real
   counts (disasters, followers, engagement) or the
   prop is visibly absurd. Never invented metrics
   presented straight.
