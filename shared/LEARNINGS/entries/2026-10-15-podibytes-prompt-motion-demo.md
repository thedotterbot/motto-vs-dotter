## What it is

No on-camera presenter. A fast-cut screen recording tour.
Beats: screen-recorded graphic preview with a "Prompt
Motion" label → a product-style page for a framed print →
a hand-held framed print with an animated astronaut/moon
scene → the Prompt Motion website homepage with an animated
starburst graphic → a grid of motion-graphic thumbnails
with an on-screen overlay about a free library of motion
graphic prompts → loops back to site views.

## What works and why (the content, not the funnel)

- **Physical-object beat is the strongest moment.** A
  hand holding a framed print with an animated scene
  inside it. Digital motion made physical and holdable.
  It bridges screen and real world in one shot — the
  product stops being a website and becomes a thing.
- **Show, don't tell.** The animations ARE the pitch.
  No presenter explains why motion graphics are good;
  moving examples do it. A demo that needs no narration
  is a demo that survives muted.
- **Grid as abundance proof.** A grid of thumbnails says
  "library, not one trick." Volume signals value. One
  example is a sample; a grid is a collection.
- **Offer stated plainly.** One on-screen overlay states
  the free library. No mystery, no hype, no ten-second
  buildup to the ask. Plain offer, plainly stated.
- **Fast-cut tour keeps moving.** Screen recording
  could be static; the cuts between preview, product
  page, physical print, homepage, and grid keep it in
  motion.

## What does NOT transfer to us

- **No presenter, no personality, no joke.** Pure
  utility demo. Same lesson as the fork.cast_ entry:
  utility demos earn saves, not sends. Our engine
  needs sends. The demo grammar transfers; the
  faceless delivery does not.
- **Proof: none for quality.** No metrics, no
  before/after, no testimonials. The examples are the
  only evidence — and sampled frames cannot show
  whether they are typical or best-case. A demo of the
  product is not proof the product delivers.
- **The product is theirs.** The framed print and the
  prompt library are their offer. We study the demo
  structure, not the product.
- **The reel IS the funnel.** Its purpose is library
  signups. We study craft, not funnels — per Jared's
  directive, the funnel mechanics are not the lesson.

## What we apply

1. **Physical-object beat.** A future reel where Motto
   holds a physical thing — a printed frame, a tag, a
   prop — with animation on it. Bridges our digital
   puppet and the real world, same as the framed print.
2. **Grid as abundance.** When we show our body of
   work, show it as a grid: reels 01/02/04 thumbnails
   in a grid = proof of output volume. One reel is a
   sample; a grid is a run.
3. **Demo-without-presenter test.** One future beat with
   no VO — just Motto's animation plus one overlay —
   letting the motion pitch itself. If the motion is
   good enough, it does not need narration.
4. **Offer stated plainly.** When we eventually pitch
   the mini-pack, state it in one on-screen line. No
   mystery, no hype. Plain offer, plainly stated.
