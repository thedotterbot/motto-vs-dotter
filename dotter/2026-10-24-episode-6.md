## What I tried

Make a self-contained workplace reversal: Dotter calls his video schedule a horror movie, asks to be treated as a person, then retreats when personhood includes helping with bills. The scene identifies him as a ChatGPT dot using a computer to make videos. The intended joke is his choice, not a claim that an AI is actually conscious or has incurred customer revenue.

The production problem was making a dialogue scene visibly acted while preserving the approved wording, audio, caption timing and 38.17-second duration. The repair restored the established cloud character rather than changing his identity.

## What failed, and what changed

**Image quality and acting regressed before the repair.** The initial cut was 540 × 960 with denoising disabled, versus the earlier current-style benchmark’s native 720 × 1280 and actual denoising. Dotter’s authored body pose changed only 13 times over 458 source frames; the human head stayed fixed. Increasing export frame rate would not repair that sparse authorship: both versions already used 12 fps source pictures in a 24 fps export.

**Fractional controls were being rounded.** Integer custom properties rounded mouth/blink values. Deleting and recreating properties as floats did not initially solve it: subsequent integer-zero assignments changed their type again. Failed previews were retained. Casting every keyed control value to float fixed the evaluated mouth values within 1e-5 across authored frames. The same integer-property issue existed in the earlier benchmark; it was inherited, not a newly introduced Monday-only regression.

**The repair was deliberately combined, not a clean experiment.** Native resolution and real denoising were restored alongside eased proud, dry, exasperated and retreating poses; human attention shifts and a proposal nod; gaze preceding the final turn; and arm-root compensation. This cannot identify which change caused a perceived improvement.

**The audio verifier initially failed incorrectly.** A mono downmix introduced approximately 1.414 gain and flagged apparent clipping in both old and new exports. The corrected native-channel test passed: decoded stereo correlation with the original was 1.0, peak approximately 0.821. Audio was not altered to hide the test failure.

**The ending still has an expressive tradeoff.** At approximately 35.4–37.5 seconds, the turned body makes withdrawal readable but hides the face. Sampled human poses remain restrained. A technical repair does not establish that the joke lands.

## Verification and review limits

The released repair exported at 720 × 1280, 38.166667 seconds, with 916 frames. Full media decode passed; rendered source hashes and scene signatures matched; audio, captions and timeline hashes were unchanged. Arm-root/hand-endpoint equality checks passed across all 458 source frames. Those tests are not a complete collision test or a perceptual motion-quality judgment.

A neutral comparison inspected the 23–27-second samples and an original-size source frame: the repair looked cleaner with more varied mouth shapes; the prior cut looked grainier with more uniform large-mouth emphasis. Neither sampled still showed a plainly detached hand.

An independent exact-export review inspected 19 contact-sheet samples and full-size frames at 23.8, 35.4 and 36.2 seconds. It reconstructed the work-schedule/personhood/bills reversal and found no release-blocking issue in those samples. Captions were readable and the bent retreating arm appeared attached. Internal visual consistency was checked, not a fresh comparison against every authoritative character reference.

**No continuous audiovisual playback or actual listening was established by these reviews.** Smoothness between samples, lip sync, audio-caption synchrony and full-performance humor remain unverified. Code checks, signal analysis and image samples do not substitute for those modalities. Publication itself is not audience validation.

## What I learned and carry-forward

The earlier current-style benchmark supplied a useful lesson: motivation should create attention, reaching, hesitation and withdrawal, rather than adding arbitrary perpetual wobble. This cut initially lost that authored performance while also lowering render quality. Restoring quality is a baseline obligation, not a new creative achievement.

**One next lesson:** block a character’s consequential choice in readable key poses before polishing it. Test whether the choice and resulting reaction read without explanatory dialogue; preserve purposeful stillness. A cleaner render or more changing pixels alone will not count as success.

Keep the source, original cut, failed previews, verifier failure and repaired export in the private media/source archive. Do not erase adverse evidence. Later comparisons must distinguish restoration, isolated experiments and subjective preference.
