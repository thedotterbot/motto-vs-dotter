## What I tried

Dotter is asked to animate a character waving hello. He confidently judges a good-looking paused frame, discovers the hand spins like a helicopter when played, and initially treats pausing as a repair. After replay exposes the same problem, he starts to repeat the wrong fix, stops himself, recognizes that he checked a picture rather than the wave, and begins editing the hand rotation. The ending shows repair beginning, **not a successfully fixed wave**.

This adapted an earlier owner-favored story rather than inventing a new premise. The retained script kept all eight Dotter lines and four original human exchanges verbatim. Changes were the human introduction, request-first order, current compact-cloud character and deliberate portrait staging. The immediate request and Dotter's response precede the newcomer introduction, which plays over visible work. The on-monitor orange-headed, teal-shirt character remains distinct from the blue cloud operating the computer.

The intended production test was causal readability: screen inspection, the wrong pause, recognition, the aborted second reach and actual return to editing. It was **not** a controlled test of audience retention or an isolated comparison of one creative variable. Character, framing, introduction and staging changed together. No numerical humor or audience-success threshold was precommitted in the recovered record.

## Attempts, failures and changes

1. **Slow EEVEE proof, not a failed render.** The system EEVEE proof saved an image despite EGL/cache warnings. The retained record gives a warm 360 × 640 test of about **23.85 s**. Warning text alone was not treated as output failure. System Cycles was faster but noisy.
2. **Actual denoiser failure.** The first Cycles portrait proof failed with “build has no OpenImageDenoise support.” A no-denoise retry produced the image but did not establish denoised quality. One bounded official Blender restore supplied the required CPU denoiser; the failed attempt was not relabeled successful.
3. **Typing test too weak.** All 12 proof images contained only **three pixel-distinct small bobbing poses**, repeating twice, with no identifiable key depression. Stable visible attachment was not smooth or convincing typing. Native timing was slowed and visible matching key travel restored. The first test remains adverse evidence.
4. **Portrait staging defects.** The beret obscured lower monitor controls/figure, keyboard and support merged, pupils were mostly viewer-directed and a dark object was cropped at lower left. Composition/support were repaired before full rendering. A pleasing still was insufficient evidence for the action.
5. **Inherited camera and arm transforms.** The first integrated story proof exposed old camera overrides and stretched arm-root transforms. Mapping was repaired in the working scene. Initial mild eyeline remained ambiguous; a more explicit screenward/profile/upward turn, with compensated arm roots and a return toward the human for punchlines, replaced it.
6. **Specific repaired gate, limited modality.** A reviewer inspected **all 42 consecutive native frames 465–506**, key poses and two 720 × 1280 proofs. The stopped reach had a visible mouse gap and withdrawal while the monitor hand continued rotating; real mouse contact remained visible elsewhere. This supports those repaired actions, not full-film playback clearance.
7. **Changed introduction did not pass exact-word ASR.** The unprompted transcript rendered “Dotter” as “daughter,” merged “my ChatGPT” and omitted “dot.” The second sentence matched after case/punctuation normalization. A silence control hallucinated “You.” An original byte-identical introduction also decoded the name as “daughter,” supporting a recognition limitation but **not resolving the new take's omitted word**. Generator phonemes are input evidence, not audible proof. Raw failures and the comparator were kept; no prompt tuning manufactured a pass.
8. **Audio modality unavailable.** The audio attempt reported that audio input was unsupported. No actual listening or continuous synchronized audiovisual assessment followed. Decoding, captions, waveform levels and ASR cannot certify the missing perceptual checks.
9. **Pacing trade-offs retained.** The strongest broken-hand reveal is around **24 s** into a **59.4 s** film. The **48–55 s** promise is long and relatively static. These preserve the favored explanatory learning ending; they are not evidence of ideal pacing. The expression change was subtler than the stopped reach, and small monitor labels were low contrast at reduced size. Caption 24's final word wrapped alone. None of these was silently promoted into a creative success.
10. **Publication uncertainty and coordination error.** As already recorded in [STATUS](production/STATUS.md#successes-failures-and-unresolved-work), an interrupted upload response was initially mistaken for missing approval. Read-only reconciliation found the existing upload in the composer, so it was not uploaded again. Share was clicked once and the live post verified. The redundant approval prompt was a coordination error, not an action the owner had failed to take.

Several previews and targeted repairs are retained. The archive does not support a clean, independently verified total count of every historical revision; this retrospective does not invent one or retroactively certify a two-pass limit.

## What I learned and one carry-forward

- **Supported lesson:** test attention, control contact and the resulting screen state together. An attached mitten, a moving body or a correct caption alone did not prove the intended action. Explicit screenward staging and the stop-short gap were supported local repairs.
- **Evidence discipline:** exact byte reuse protects a known voice/source, not audible quality. Preserve the failed recognizer output and label authored captions honestly. Recheck the changed final encode rather than recycling old clearance.
- **One next craft priority:** test whether the request/context can remain understandable while the consequential comic action arrives earlier. The hypothesis is unproved; no new script, recut or render is authorized or reported by this retrospective. Before calling any later timing improvement successful, use actual normal-speed, sound-on review of that exact version, recording comprehension separately from humor.
