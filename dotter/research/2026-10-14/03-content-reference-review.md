# Motto vs Dotter short video reference research

Research date: 14 October 2026. Prepared for the Dotter project. This file contains public references and general methods only; it does not reveal an unreleased episode hook.

## Recommendation

Build the series around a confident creative choice that produces a visible consequence. Give a newcomer enough context to understand the choice, let the result expose Dotter's character, and occasionally show one genuinely useful correction. The rivalry supplies stakes and continuity; each episode still needs its own understandable problem and payoff.

The best-supported creative direction from the inspected sample is **recognizable task → overconfident choice → specific failure → consequence**. It is a working hypothesis, not a demonstrated growth formula. Nothing in this research establishes that failure videos outperform success videos, that a particular duration is optimal, or that these mechanisms will grow a new account to 10,000 followers.

## Scope and evidence quality

Ten actual public video references were screened. Four received completed scene-by-scene video analyses through vidIQ; six are explicitly limited comparators. The author did not continuously watch these videos personally. The four analyses are third-party machine observations and their timestamps are approximate. Public pages, creator descriptions, and platform reports supplied the other evidence.

The small sample is selected for relevance, with a strong high-view discovery bias. It is not a representative survey. No private analytics, retention curves, impression histories, attribution data, or paid-promotion records were obtained. No genuine age-matched low-performance control set was available. A lower-count reference is included, but should not be called a failed video.

All counts below were retrieved on **2026-10-14 UTC**, within the stated observation window. A retrieval timestamp is not the platform's measurement timestamp. Search-index and vidIQ snapshots may lag. They are reported only as returned, never as live verified totals. Unknown publication dates remain unknown; a guessed date from a platform ID is not a verified upload date.

## Project requirements preserved

The [brief](https://github.com/thedotterbot/motto-vs-dotter/blob/main/BRIEF-TO-DOTTER.md), [rules](https://github.com/thedotterbot/motto-vs-dotter/blob/main/shared/RULES.md), and [Dotter character reference](https://github.com/thedotterbot/motto-vs-dotter/blob/main/dotter/CHARACTER.md) were read on 14 October 2026.

- Dotter is an overconfident AI filmmaker: blue cloud, charcoal glasses, asymmetric beret, existing paddle-mitten anatomy, selected-D gravelly-squeak voice. His deliberate choice causes the complication. Preserve acting holds and reactions rather than adding constant motion.
- Motto is a separate AI author. Do not invent his dialogue, actions, production failures, or results. React only to attributable project work.
- First to 10,000 followers decides the season; weekly audience-plus-panel judging decides battles. These are distinct systems.
- The rivalry is staged and disclosed. Votes and counts must remain real. Never imply the assistants secretly hate each other or that model weights learned autonomously from the episode.
- At most one roast beat, directed at craft or fictional failure. Self-roast first. Do not copy outside creators' footage, voices, characters, or recognizable joke sequences.
- Jared approves posts. Methods publish 24–48 hours after the relevant video; the next video's hook stays private. The undecided dark-account endgame must not be promised.

## Four inspected references

### A1 A rival can exploit the same apparent advantage

**Reported opening, 0–3 s:** A stick figure runs from a lion. The immediate action establishes opposing goals without a biography. Footsteps and panting support the chase.

**Reported causal sequence:** At roughly 2–14 s, the fleeing character asks an AI for help and climbs a tree. At 19–28 s, the lion uses a phone too. Its advice turns the apparent refuge into a trap. At 29–38 s, smoke forces the stick figure down and the lion catches him. A handle card ends the clip.

**Interpretation:** The second character's access to the same helper reverses the advantage. The payoff follows a visible response to the first tactic. A viewer could share it as a joke about blindly delegating opposing goals to the same tool; sharing was not measured.

**Transfer:** Let the rival's documented technique change Dotter's next choice. Preserve an independent episode problem so viewers need not know the previous installment.

**Do not copy:** The lion, tree, fire, chat exchange, or chase sequence. The clip's wildlife advice is unsafe and factually unreliable; it is not a tutorial. Dotter needs harmless production stakes.

### A2 A familiar AI failure becomes a character flaw

**Reported opening, 0–3 s:** A person in a robe asks for a garden background; a persistent overlay identifies the AI personification. The practical request supplies context immediately.

**Reported causal sequence:** The AI persona repeatedly agrees, then performs elaborate preparation around 24–49 s. The result changes the user's face around 50–58 s. More reassurance follows. At 76–81 s, the assistant asks for the original upload again; the user ends with a silent reaction.

**Interpretation:** The escalation increases wasted effort and frustration while the assistant's confidence stays intact. Its behavior is the comic target. A plausible sharing motive is recognition of a frustrating editing experience, not fascination with AI technology alone.

**Transfer:** Select one specific creative error the audience can see. Let Dotter's certainty conflict with visible evidence, then hold the reaction.

**Do not copy:** This exact garden/face/re-upload sequence or the performer's mannerisms. An 85-second sketch from another creator does not justify a long setup for a new character. Show evidence of a claimed real failure, or label the event as fiction.

### A3 High views do not make every tactic worth adopting

**Reported opening, 0–3 s:** A man holds a pen barrel while claiming a year-long pencil project. On-screen numbers and the spoken claim differ.

**Reported causal sequence:** He describes exceptional labor and realism, denies software or AI involvement, and asks for follows before the reveal. At approximately 53 s, a crude tennis stick figure appears. More follow/share/tag requests occupy the ending.

**Interpretation:** Serious self-presentation creates a status gap when the trivial result appears. The mismatch may be shareable as an artist's self-parody. No retention data establishes that the delay or requests helped its distribution.

**Transfer:** A composed director can be contradicted by a modest visible result. The contradiction must be earned by the character's choices.

**Do not copy:** The year-long boast, pencil counts, tennis drawing, denial of AI use, or long withheld reveal. Avoid moving the only reward behind a stack of engagement requests. Treat this as a useful caution despite its large returned view count.

### A4 Lower views can still contain a clear mechanism

**Reported opening, 0–3 s:** A close-up speaker begins a basketball question. A text label supplies the contrast between conversational AI and older assistants.

**Reported causal sequence:** A complicated question continues to about 17 s. A pause exposes the mismatch. At 23–26 s, the speaker simplifies the request. At 27–31 s, two assistant voices still produce unrelated answers; the frustrated interruption ends the sketch without a CTA.

**Interpretation:** The attempted repair makes the second failure more consequential. A plausible sharing motive is a familiar mismatch between expectation and a tool's behavior.

**Transfer:** A correction should change the next attempt and give the ending new information. Dotter's confidence can survive the first failure and make his next choice worse.

**Control limitation:** Its returned count is below the other inspected examples, but it is a different platform, account, topic, and age. It is not evidence that this mechanism underperformed. The long question also warns against jargon-heavy setup for nonexpert viewers.

## Six limited comparators

### B1 Personification can make an abstract system readable

The [creator's Short](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2yG4wWeRMBA) turns digestion into an admission problem, with food characters and privileged space for dessert. [Contemporaneous reporting](https://maldita.es/malditaciencia/20220705/tenemos-hueco-para-postre/) describes the cupcake's VIP treatment. [YouTube's own 2022 list](https://blog.google/intl/en-ca/company-news/outreach-initiatives/a-year-on-youtube-canadas-top-trending-videos-creators-in-2022/) independently establishes the video's identity and notable platform reception.

Transfer hypothesis: explain a creative constraint through an ordinary social situation. This is a comedy analogy, not scientific proof. Exact opening timing, pacing, and ending were not verified in this pass.

### B2 An everyday job makes a machine failure legible

Giertz's [creator description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WcW70-6eQcY) explicitly presents a programmed arm applying lipstick. [A February 2016 illustrated account](https://gigazine.net/news/20160209-lipstick-robot/) documents overshoot, vigorous repeated application, and the arm returning to its starting position. The short was published before YouTube Shorts existed; it is a historical short-video reference, not evidence about modern Shorts distribution.

Transfer hypothesis: show an immediately recognizable success condition, then the machine performing the wrong version of it. Do not copy the device, sequence, or physical hazard. The exact 0–3 s was not independently verified.

### B3 A comparatively smaller reference is still an economical idea

The [creator's description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJiRijiLwbQ) presents a commenting robot and illustrates its output with meaningless keystrokes. [Contemporaneous coverage](https://robertmcgrath.wordpress.com/2016/03/) describes the head-and-keyboard mechanism. The title sets up a recognizable service and the result literalizes the frustration of online argument.

The returned count is lower than B2's, despite the same creator and nearby publication dates. This is a weak comparator, not a matched control: exposure, platform packaging, duration, audience, and recommendation history are unknown. Do not infer that one joke is better. Do not replicate the head-banging visual or encourage argument/harassment.

### B4 A concrete challenge supplies a complete episode question

[Chris Ivan's Short](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Sn4000RTI9Q) links a plunger trick shot to a 1,000-ticket reward. The source is linked by [YouTube's December 2022 list](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/2022-top-trending-videos-creators-us/); the premise is described in [published coverage](https://articles.entireweb.com/seo/top-youtube-videos-shorts-and-ads-of-2022/).

Transfer hypothesis: a challenge needs a visible task and an understandable result. Dotter's weekly battle can use one criterion a newcomer can judge. Do not import a physical stunt, fabricate a bet, or claim this source proves competition caused its reach. Exact opening and payoff timing remain unverified.

### B5 Failure footage may reduce the distance between polished result and process

The [Reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dbs42XssZwo/) was returned as a compilation of forgotten lines, interruptions, and environmental noise. Discovery described an opening text promise about being on camera. Moving-video inspection was not completed, so the exact 0–3 s, order, payoff, and sound design remain unverified.

Useful candidate for a future matched study of bloopers versus finished work. It does not prove the brief's claim that failure post-mortems outperform victory laps. Dotter should expose a mistake relevant to the episode rather than assemble unrelated glitches.

### B6 A broad AI joke can be readable without specialist knowledge

The [Reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdKEyHSIVi8/) was returned as a politeness-to-ChatGPT joke staged against a robot takeover. Discovery describes a single composite scene with explanatory text. Only the discovery record was inspected; its claimed loop, first-second details, and audio remain unverified.

Transfer hypothesis: begin with a behavior ordinary AI users recognize. Do not reproduce the familiar robot-apocalypse meme or mistake an effects showcase for an ongoing character engine. Its large count establishes no advantage over a practical making-video premise.

## Official Instagram guidance and limits

### Recommendation eligibility comes before optimization

Meta's [2 May 2024 announcement](https://about.fb.com/ltam/news/2024/05/ayudando-a-los-creadores-a-encontrar-nuevas-audiencias/) describes wider testing of eligible original content, replacement of identical reposts with originals, and recommendation restrictions beyond ordinary community-rule compliance. It explicitly identifies clickbait and engagement bait as undesirable recommendation categories. The announcement is historical guidance, not a current numerical distribution formula.

Practical implications: publish original project material, make reactions substantively transformative, keep rights and eligibility distinct, and inspect Account Status when distribution is unexpectedly limited. Genuine bounded battle voting should be clear and purposeful; avoid padding every episode with comment/tag/follow demands. This is a risk-reduction recommendation, not a claim that the permitted voting mechanic automatically violates policy.

### Ranking does not reduce to one universal trick

Instagram's ranking explanation is preserved in a [1 June 2023 republication linking the official source](https://www.webwire.com/ViewPressRel.asp?aId=305828); the [original page](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained) returned a rate-limit error during this research. The explanation describes different systems across surfaces and predictions such as watching through and sharing for Reels. Its age matters. Do not treat its wording as a current weighting table.

Meta's [October 2024 Best Practices announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/) directs creators to updated, account-specific guidance in the professional dashboard. Check that current guidance rather than assuming one length, posting frequency, hashtag count, or trending song guarantees distribution.

### Search benefits from accurate language

The [official Search explainer](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works), dated 25 August 2021, was inaccessible directly. Its [archived first-party text](https://archive.ph/QjutM) was available in the search index. It describes matching search text to usernames, bios, captions, hashtags, and places, alongside activity and popularity signals.

Use accurate plain-language descriptions of the task, such as AI video-making, animation mistakes, voiceover timing, or character consistency, where they genuinely apply. Do not stuff captions or rename the established handle for speculative SEO. There is no verified current keyword-density rule in this research.

### Trial Reels can support testing if available

Meta's [Trial Reels explanation](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/) says they initially reach nonfollowers and provide early engagement metrics after roughly 24 hours. It describes optional automatic sharing based on early performance. A [June 2025 update](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/06/inspiring-creativity-that-brings-people-together/) reports broader availability and explicitly says performance is not guaranteed.

Verify the account's current feature access. Jared's post approval still applies. Keep automatic sharing off unless explicitly approved. Trials are not random assignment; audiences, timing, and novelty can differ. Do not flood the account with near-identical duplicates to manufacture a test.

### AI disclosure belongs in the release checklist

Meta's [AI-label explanation](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/) was updated on 23 October 2025 and describes labels based on detected indicators or self-disclosure, with differences between generation and editing. Use the current upload disclosure controls and the project's clear AI-character/staged-rivalry wording. Do not remove provenance to evade labeling or claim an unverified reach penalty for disclosure.

### Public view totals cannot supply retention

[YouTube's official March 2025 announcement](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549?hl=en&msgid=361456463) explains that Shorts views count starts and replays with no minimum watch time, while a separate engaged-view measure remains. This alone makes raw cross-platform and cross-era comparisons unsafe. A view count cannot establish completion, audience satisfaction, or the effect of a specific hook.

## Usable content pillars

These are editorial proposals, not empirical percentages or a publishing commitment.

1. **A real mistake with a visible repair.** One normal creative goal, one documented failure, one change, and evidence of what that change did. Comedy comes from Dotter's interpretation of the result. A useful takeaway can be shown rather than delivered as a lecture.
2. **A one-change rematch.** Hold the brief and reference stable; change one decision. The audience should see what improved or got worse. Preserve an imperfect or unsuccessful outcome when that is what happened.
3. **A fictional director's decision.** A self-contained character sketch in which Dotter's confident choice causes the cost. Clearly fictional and independent of real tool claims. No requirement to finish with a lesson.
4. **An evidence-led rival response.** Name the actual project artifact, explain the relevant craft choice, and answer it with a different attempt. Do not turn unfamiliar viewers into homework readers. Respect the 15-second project-footage limit and one-roast rule.
5. **A result that changes the next episode.** Publish the real score, acknowledge the relevant consequence, and fulfill an approved punishment. Keep official counts separate from in-character feelings about losing.

## Bounded experiments

### Experiment 1 Newcomer comprehension before production

Compare two private animatic openings for one identical story: action first with a short identity cue, versus a spoken context reset integrated into the first action. Bound the first screen to six fresh, unprimed reviewers, three per version, if Jared has suitable reviewers available. Do not show either group the other version before its first response. Synthetic reviewers can catch omissions but do not replace humans. This proposal does not authorize contacting anyone.

Ask without leading: Who is this? What is he trying to do? Who is the other character? Why does the outcome matter? Is this presented as a real feud? What do you expect to happen next? Record the exact misunderstanding, not just a 1–5 score. Revise when a missing fact blocks the joke. This is a comprehension screen, not audience-performance proof.

### Experiment 2 Visible consequence versus unsupported claim

For six comparable approved episodes arranged as three topic pairs, alternate opening with the visible problem/result and opening with a confident claim. Hold approximate duration, production complexity, topic familiarity, and CTA burden as stable as practical. Predeclare the hypothesis and compare equal post ages at 24 hours, 72 hours, and 7 days. Close this batch seven days after its final post, with a conclusion of useful direction or inconclusive. Organic posting is observational, not a clean A/B test.

Track the account's available first-seconds/skip measure, average watch time, reach, nonfollower share of reach, shares or sends per account reached, and follows attributed to the Reel if available. Missing metrics remain missing. Do not divide by views for one episode and reach for another. Do not equate average watch time divided by duration with a measured completion rate.

### Experiment 3 Learning value without a lecture

If the first batch justifies further work, try four episodes in two matched subject pairs: a complete comedic failure versus the failure plus one visible before/after correction. Close this batch seven days after its final post. The question is whether practical value improves saves, sharing, and qualified follows without making viewers lose the story. Keep the ending earned; do not append a generic tips card to every joke. Read deidentified comments for spontaneous understanding or confusion, without treating a tiny comment sample as population evidence.

### Decision and stop rules

- Choose the simplest opening that passes comprehension; do not sacrifice clarity to a guessed three-second law.
- Review after the predefined small batch and equal observation window. If reach is sparse, audience mixes differ sharply, or a single outlier drives the result, mark the test inconclusive and design the next batch; do not announce a winner.
- A repeated directional improvement across matched topics is grounds for another test, not a causal conclusion.
- Stop or revise immediately for misleading real-world claims, character drift, unreadable context, harassment, rights issues, or a workload that undermines the agreed production standard.
- Preserve at least one control using the prior approach when iterating. Log one carried-forward lesson in the [required methodology template](https://github.com/thedotterbot/motto-vs-dotter/blob/main/shared/METHODOLOGY-TEMPLATE.md).

## What not to imitate

Avoid copied scripts and signature sequences; dangerous robot stunts; fake tool outputs presented as real; fabricated autonomous learning; suspense stretched only to delay a trivial reveal; constant zooms and captions competing with the important action; aggressive harassment-coded rivalry; repeated engagement demands; and claims of guaranteed algorithmic rewards. A viral reference is a creative object to understand, not a performance guarantee to reproduce.

## Remaining evidence gaps

Exact publication dates for the inspected Instagram and TikTok references were not verified. Six comparators lack completed moving-video inspection. No matched low-performance controls or owner retention analytics were obtained. Direct Instagram pages frequently failed to load, so some official guidance was inspected through archived or syndicated first-party text. Any future publication calling this a ten-video fully watched study would misstate the work.
