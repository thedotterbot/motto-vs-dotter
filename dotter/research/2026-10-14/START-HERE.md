# Dotter growth strategy: entertain first, prove something useful

Research and recommendations, 14 October 2026. These are proposed experiments, not measured audience results or a promise of viral growth.

## The decision

Build a character-comedy show for people curious about creating with AI. Make each short enjoyable without knowing Dotter, Motto, Muse, or dots. Give interested creators a small, reproducible improvement after the joke. The rivalry earns attention; the recurring characters and useful evidence give people a reason to return.

Do not aim exclusively at current dot/Muse owners. Access, subscriptions and geography narrow that audience. Nor should this become a generic AI-news or prompt-pack feed. Our strongest working hypothesis is a creative beginner who wants one finished, recognizable video rather than another complicated stack.

## Who we are serving

1. **Primary practical audience:** beginner creators trying to use an assistant as a creative partner. Their job is finishing something worth showing with fewer wasted takes, credits and explanations.
2. **Secondary practical audience:** independent character/mascot-video makers who struggle with continuity, timing, voice and coherent stories. They may use neither product.
3. **Discovery audience:** people entertained by an overconfident AI character and a transparent competition. They do not need to download anything or become customers to be valuable viewers.

These groups overlap. A handful of self-selected discussions supports the problems; it does not establish market size, willingness to pay, or our audience fit. Followers, freebie users and buyers must be counted separately.

## The content promise

“Watch two AI characters try to make better videos. Enjoy the mistakes, judge the results, and borrow the useful fixes.”

Every episode needs an immediately legible goal and a choice that causes what happens next. Reintroduce the character through that action, without spending the opening reciting the whole season. A brief identity label and the current goal can provide context; the dialogue still needs to work for a stranger. The payoff comes before a download or follow request.

Start with a provisional mix of two comedy attempts and one visible repair/comparison per week, only when production quality and owner approval allow. Treat this as a capacity hypothesis, not an automatic publishing commitment. Weekly battles and reactions should have their own purpose rather than fill a quota.

Public methods should show the actual defect, the changed variable and the checked result. Keep future episode hooks private and honor the existing delayed-methods rule. Rivalry votes are not a scientific comparison of underlying AI models.

## What to build first

**One small character-short starter kit**, with an ordinary-chat/manual path and optional agent adapters:

- Character reference and a concise identity-lock card
- Three-shot worksheet: goal, choice, consequence
- Timing/caption worksheet and review checklist
- One worked example, actual before/after exports, and the documented fix
- Clear costs, required assets, reuse rights, supported editor/version and fallback

The text template and fictional exercise accompanying this report are drafts. They are not yet a tested, complete giveaway. Before promoting it, finish the sample media and have an unfamiliar person complete the exercise without a paid agent, surprise trial or missing file.

Release the useful file ungated on the existing project site/repo. Do not ask for an email or automate DMs just to deliver a worksheet. Add an optional, minimal feedback path later if authorized. A download is interest; an example of someone using it is stronger evidence.

Second: a creator handoff card. Third: an honest cost-and-repair log. Defer a giant prompt library and a full autonomous creator installation until actual users demonstrate a need.

## Setup for the audience

- Lowest-friction path: owned images, recorded voice and a simple editor. The first result can be a short animatic, not expensive generated motion.
- Phone users: test a core Edits workflow before recommending exact steps; account requirements and paid extras must be explicit.
- Desktop users: a tested Shotcut/Kdenlive path can avoid generative-service dependency.
- Existing agent owners: use the assistant for planning, asset consistency, a cheap preview, review and packaging. Identify tool access and cost before generating a batch.

Do not recommend our entire internal production stack as the beginner setup. The user needs a finished result, not our maintenance burden.

## Discoverability and profile

The saved @dotterbot bio states identity, goal and rivalry stakes. The suggested display name is “Dotter | ChatGPT & AI Video”; a descriptive name helps people understand the topic but is no ranking guarantee. The website should explain the premise, show both characters, and eventually offer a clear useful resource.

Use natural topic words in captions and relevant descriptions. Avoid keyword stuffing, unrelated hashtags, fake engagement, mass tagging or manufactured conflict. Never imply official OpenAI/Meta sponsorship. Profile/link discoverability is hygiene; a clear, satisfying video is the creative priority.

## How we learn

Record native Insights at comparable ages: 24 hours, 72 hours and seven days. Keep actual counts, exact platform labels and missing values. Begin manually; Metricool currently has no connected social network, and automation/API coverage differs from the app.

The small scorecard is:

- Reach and non-follower discovery when actually exposed
- Native average watch time; skip rate when available
- Shares and saves per 1,000 reached
- Native Reel-attributed follows per 1,000 reached
- Weekly public follower count for the official race
- Freebie delivery and separate evidence of use
- Production time/cost and newcomer clarity

Watch-time ratio is not completion rate. UTM tags do not collect analytics by themselves. Vote comments are not the same as substantive engagement. Never fill unavailable metrics with zero.

First test one opening variable across comparable original episodes. Use days 7, 14 and 30 to check collection, inspect provisional patterns and make an evidence-bound decision. Small samples remain inconclusive; one breakout does not prove a formula. The measurement appendix defines proposed practical thresholds and their limits.

## What “going viral” can honestly mean

We cannot engineer a guaranteed breakout. We can increase the opportunity by making the opening understandable, delivering a causal visual joke, giving a specific reason to share, and consistently improving the weakest observed element. If an episode breaks out, follow it with a fresh story serving the same audience need, not a series of near-duplicates. Assess both distribution and follow/use yield; views alone can be empty attention.
