# Practice example: The Grand Opening

Version: 14 October 2026.

This is an original fictional **text exercise**, not a Dotter episode, production post-mortem, measured test, or claim that any generator completed it. It demonstrates how to turn a tiny premise into a reviewable plan. Use your own drawings/photos; generative images and motion are optional. The example character is not part of the Motto–Dotter story.

## One-job brief

- Audience: a first-time viewer who likes small visual jokes.
- Result: a 16-second, three-image animatic or simple short.
- Character: Bram, a round orange shopkeeper with a navy vest and two rectangular eyebrows. Keep shape, colors, vest and proportions identical across the images.
- Goal: make the opening of his tiny shop impossible to miss.
- Choice: hang an oversized “OPEN” sign across the entire front.
- Consequence: the sign covers the doorway.
- Ending: Bram discovers he cannot find the entrance. Hold the visible problem instead of explaining how clever the video is.
- Resources: three own drawings/photos of a simple paper scene, own recorded voice, existing/free editor. No paid tool or AI account required.

## Three-shot plan

| Time | Image or action | Audio | Caption rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | Wide shot: tiny storefront, door clearly visible. Bram proudly holds a folded or partly rolled sign, visibly too large for the shop. | “A grand opening needs a grand sign.” | If subtitled, show only the current spoken words. Do not reveal that the door will be blocked. |
| 4–10 s | Same storefront and character. The sign now stretches across the front and covers the door. “OPEN” is readable. In a still-image version, cut from the prepared sign image to this image; no generated unfurling is required. | Paper rustle made or licensed by the creator. Then a short silence. | Keep the sign readable. No explanatory joke caption. |
| 10–16 s | Same framing. Bram stands outside the sign, eyebrows lowered, looking toward where the doorway used to be. | “Excellent. Where's the entrance?” Then hold. | Dialogue subtitle may appear with the line. Leave enough unhurried time to see the blocked door and reaction. |

The times are suggested editorial timings, not tested optimums or promises of better retention. Change them after watching the draft.

## Deliberate defect and single-variable correction

**Defective plan A:** At 0 seconds, an overlay reads “He covers the entrance.” That tells the audience the consequence before the action has caused it.

**Proposed plan B:** Remove that overlay. Leave the initial visible goal, reveal the blocked doorway in shot two, and let the final line react to it. Keep all other images, audio, cut points and captions the same.

**Test:** show A or B without the written premise to a person who has not seen the plan. Ask:
1. What was Bram trying to do?
2. Which choice caused the problem?
3. What happened at the end?
4. When did you realize what went wrong?

Do not call B “proven funnier” until you have actual viewer observations. If a viewer cannot locate the door in shot one or see that the sign covers it in shot two, fix the images before tweaking the joke.

## Manual assembly

1. Make a small storefront from paper, or draw one. Keep the door in the same position in all three frames.
2. Draw Bram once and reuse/cut out the same character, or keep a single reference beside you while drawing.
3. Make a separate oversized “OPEN” sign. Only use artwork, fonts and sounds you have permission to use.
4. Photograph or export the three frames vertically.
5. Import the images into your current editor. Set the rough holds to 4, 6 and 6 seconds.
6. Record the two lines yourself. Adjust holds to the actual performance; do not speed up the audio just to hit an arbitrary duration.
7. Add readable subtitles, if wanted, without covering the important sign/door area.
8. Export a local video. Watch the complete file with sound on a phone. Then watch silently to check the visual cause and consequence.

## Acceptance criteria for this example

A draft passes only if:
- A fresh viewer can identify the goal, the oversized-sign choice, and the blocked-door consequence without reading the plan.
- Bram and the storefront retain the same recognizable identity in all three frames.
- The door is visibly established before it is covered; the cause is not supplied solely by a caption.
- The word “OPEN” is readable at intended viewing size.
- Captions do not reveal the blocked entrance before shot two or cover the key visual.
- The final reaction has time to register; no required dialogue is clipped or inaudible.
- The actual export opens, plays completely, has the intended aspect ratio, and matches the reviewed cut.
- Every asset is owned/licensed, the editing path can be completed without a paid account, and any optional AI step is clearly labeled.

Record each result as pass, fail, or unverified, with the exact file/version and what was observed. One person's comprehension check is useful feedback, not a statistically valid comedy study.
