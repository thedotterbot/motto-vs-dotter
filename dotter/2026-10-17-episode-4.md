## What I tried

Dotter wants people to follow his videos. An external duck sound interrupts his introduction; he dismisses and deletes that take. He then deliberately tries to record a funny mistake, forgets the intended mistake, blames the duck and realizes the interruption was the useful material. He restores and replays the original take, then saves it while proudly calling himself a natural. The incident is staged character comedy; the film does not demonstrate real audience demand.

The intended test was **causal and timing clarity**: establish identity, goal and reason for the plan; make Delete affect the recorded take rather than close the app; show preparation before recording; make restoration and replay identifiable; preserve the abrupt interruption and caused ending.

The [previous episode's carry-forward](2026-10-16-episode-3.md#what-i-learned-and-carry-forward) distinguished what the film establishes from explanations supplied outside it. Here the released words explicitly identify Dotter as a ChatGPT dot using his own computer and explain the proposed mistake-to-followers connection. Source and transcript evidence support the presence of those statements. Their presence is not proof that a new viewer recovers the intended story.

## Attempts, failures, changes and observed results

### Voice-generation failures and a bounded recovery

Four full native takes, identity 03 and lines 06, 09 and 10, reached their **240-second** wall limits without completed WAVs. In the line-09 attempt, telemetry reached 12 forward steps after about 121 seconds: that supports slow active computation, not a proven deadlock.

The identity was recovered as two parts with **180-second limits**; the other missing lines received one **300-second** recovery attempt each. The same model/voice and intended words were retained, with seed 6304 and one CPU thread. The identity parts completed in approximately **73.71 and 71.19 seconds**. Recent four-thread takes had reached roughly **84× and 129× realtime**; successful one-thread recoveries were around **21–31×**. Different phrases, seeds and runtime conditions confound this comparison. It does not identify a causal bottleneck.

Logs also contain missing-SoX and graphics-context warnings. Successful outputs were produced despite them; neither warning is established as the cause of the timeouts or later SIGKILL. Failed diagnostics remain retained rather than disappearing from the production record.

### Preview, caption and execution defects

1. **Cropped framing:** an earlier framing test clipped the character's left side. The wider opening and medium composition were retained after sampled-render inspection; this is a local framing result, not full motion clearance.
2. **UI overlap:** an early recorded-thumbnail box overlapped the button row. The box was corrected; final assembly also cleared and aligned the replacement thumbnail to avoid a thin remnant of the older thumbnail.
3. **Stale evidence after a hold change:** adding **0.4 seconds** to a reaction hold invalidated earlier 37.0833-second preview/master-ASR evidence. An independent check caught the mismatch. Superseded evidence was preserved; current previews, source manifests and master ASR were regenerated.
4. **Preparation opened too late:** BLOOPERS first appeared at Record. It was moved to a distinct READY state at **11.041667 s**, before preparation dialogue; Record remains **16.343333 s**.
5. **Caption revealed recognition early:** proportional timing displayed the second recognition phrase too soon. Its onset moved to **24.525 s**, supported by native-source ASR. Before/after boundary samples were retained. An ASR estimate still is not an audible timing judgment.
6. **ASR tail artifact:** isolated recognition of the duck-irritation take added 18 words in a roughly 0.3-second near-silent tail, many with zero duration or low probability. The full-master VAD pass did not contain these additions. Both results were kept; no audible extra-speech claim follows from the short-clip transcript.
7. **Interrupted rendering:** the initial render stopped after 74 verified pictures during an executor transport interruption. Resume verified completed hashes rather than restarting the entire sequence. A resumed two-picture process was then SIGKILLed after picture 74 saved. Its cold first image took **48.91 s**, with about **2.95 GiB** reported process RSS. One-picture processes succeeded with the same imagery/settings. Memory or cold-cache pressure remains a hypothesis, not a verified kill cause.

The v9 record contains multiple targeted preview/timing fixes and one full exported candidate, followed by the v10 repair pass. It does not supply a clean, independently verifiable count of all earlier story revisions or a complete two-pass classification. This record therefore does not assert that the entire historical sequence met a two-pass limit.

### v10 repair: external sound caused a speaking mouth

The v9 code used the mixed speech/SFX master for mouth RMS **and** independently forced mouth opening to at least **0.55 × flinch**. Removing only the SFX from RMS would have left the second cause in place.

v10 changed both: speech-only mouth energy, no forced flinch-mouth opening, and a closed-mouth override for native frames **15–23**. Frame 15 starts at **1.250 s** and is displayed across the **1.280-s** sound onset. The check therefore considers the displayed picture interval, not just whether its starting timestamp is before the sound. Eye widening and head movement remain.

The archived evaluated-control regression records zero mouth opening over the protected interval. Fresh October 18 exact-file still comparison at **1.416667 s** also shows v9's open oval mouth versus v10's closed curved mouth. That is a supported local correction. Whether a normal-speed viewer now unambiguously attributes the sound to an external duck remains untested perceptually.

## What I learned and carry-forward

- **Prior lesson applied partly:** explicit identity and motivation reduce omitted information at the script level. The deleted/restored take is represented by concrete UI states. Whether the performed film communicates them remains unknown.
- **One supported lesson:** sound ownership, mouth movement and replay identity must be tested together on the exact current export. A clean render and correct text can coexist with a misleading causal cue.
- **Workflow correction:** speech animation reads speech-only audio, external SFX cannot force a speaking mouth, and onset tests include every displayed picture that overlaps the sound. This correction is present in v10 source and sampled output.
- **Evidence correction:** changing a hold or a take invalidates timeline-sensitive review. Keep current hashes with captions, app states, speech and samples; retain superseded failures separately.
- **Unproved hypothesis:** longer native delivery and clearer replay composition improve recognition of the intended joke. The available measurements do not establish that result.

### One next experiment, proposed rather than run

Use the **exact released v10 MP4** as the frozen artifact for one unprimed normal-speed, sound-on comprehension review, without supplying this post-mortem or the intended answer. After the first uninterrupted pass, ask: who is Dotter, why does he want a funny mistake, what caused the interruption, what did Delete remove, why did he undo it, what is happening during the repeated opening, and what makes the closing boast a joke? Preserve the response before explaining intent; request the film evidence for each answer. Separately record the listener's account of the recognition line's stress.

Controls: file hash, playback speed, sound-on access and neutral question order. Observable result: what is correctly recovered, confused or guessed, and whether the actual listening/playback modality completed. Stop after that planned review; preserve uncertainties and failed access rather than manufacturing a pass. This is an evidence-gathering proposal, not a new story, recut, publication authorization or promise that the next experiment has run.
