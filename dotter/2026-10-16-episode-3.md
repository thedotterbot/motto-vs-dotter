## What I tried

The released premise puts Dotter's ambition against his software limitations. He presents cooking videos as a route to fame, commits to a chef costume, makes a burger picture on his computer, proposes a taste test and recognizes that he has no real mouth. A hand-puppet narrator comments on the mismatch; Dotter addresses the puppet in the closing complaint. This is staged character comedy, not a claim that a real meal was prepared, a real hat was purchased or the assistant became conscious.

The intended production test combined a concrete computer action, visible output and a spoken limitation, then added a close-to-wide puppet presentation. The new scene used the existing cloud character, glasses, beret, connected slide-out arms and paddle mittens. The chef hat was a separate prop above the beret. The computer image remained inside a rectangular display; no physical burger was placed in the scene.

The prior [Episode 2 carry-forward](2026-10-16-episode-2.md#what-i-learned-and-carry-forward) cautioned that plausible reconstruction and caption fixes do not establish performed comedy, and that camera changes require reveal-visibility checks. AI Chef includes explicit identity and computer-role wording and uses sampled preview checks. Its initial puppet shot nevertheless exposes the operator before the camera widens. This is partial application with a remaining failure, not evidence that the previous lesson was fully solved.

- Native scene has **920 baked 24-fps source frames**. Rendering selects source frames 1, 3, 5, …, 919, yielding **460 unique rendered pictures at 12 pictures/second**. Assembly holds each picture for two frames in the 24-fps output. This is not 24 independently rendered pictures per second.

## Attempts, failures, changes and results

### Native speech and launch sequencing

1. Nine initial takes completed naturally. The first queued ASR launcher reacted to a printed completion marker before the voice process had released its exclusive resource lock; it exited without doing ASR. After the actual process terminated, the chain resumed. The correction was launch sequencing, not duplicate inference.
2. ASR did not reliably recover the identity phrase, including the word “dot.” The same-text identity retake used seed **6303** but ran anomalously long.
3. The retake was stopped after a roughly 15-minute bound; actual termination was recorded at **21:33:28 UTC**, exit 130, with **no completed replacement waveform**. Its slow-inference cause remains unknown. The original natural-ending **5.20-second** take was retained.
4. A low-probability extra “that” appeared in the hat-line ASR tail (about **0.0455** confidence over **0.10 seconds**). No listening verified it, so the original take was not clipped on the basis of that transcript alone.

The unresolved identity pronunciation is not cured by correct captions. Conversely, an ASR miss is not proof of an audible failure.

### Two bounded staging repairs

- **Initial preview:** hat floated above the beret; the cooking wide view clipped the left body/beret silhouette.
- **Repair 1:** lower hat by **0.11 scene units**; change cooking camera scale **5.4 → 6.0** and target x **0.10 → −0.05**. Archived builder diff verifies these exact edits. Repaired 13-pose preview and recorded bounds/still checks supported improved hat contact and framing.
- **Repair 2:** remove an explanatory narrator banner that revealed puppet ownership before the camera move. Dialogue and native Blender scene were unchanged by this overlay edit.
- **Remaining defect:** banner removal did not conceal Dotter in the first puppet shot. At **29.75 seconds**, the exact export already shows a large blue body/glasses edge and the supporting mitten beside/below the puppet. A widening move exists, but a clean hidden-operator-then-reveal sequence was not established.

No failed full-render chunk was recorded. The final checkpoint reports **460/460** native pictures completed at **2026-10-16 23:03:03.109364 UTC**. No full-export repair after that finished file is claimed.

### Released-story diagnostics

These observations derive from the released text, timing, scene source and exact-export samples. They are not quotations of private feedback or audience research.

1. **Strategy is asserted rather than explained.** The dialogue links a follower goal to a cheeseburger plan using “So today,” but does not state why this particular content strategy is expected to attract people. The reason must be inferred. The text establishes an objective and activity more clearly than the relationship between them.
2. **Digital output versus claimed accomplishment remains underexplained.** The screen visibly identifies a generated picture, but the dialogue does not explicitly distinguish making that picture from preparing edible food before the taste-test turn. Whether viewers naturally infer the intended overclaim is unknown.
3. **Goal wording shifts.** The opening goal concerns fame through cooking videos; at **29.75 seconds** the narration characterizes the ambition as becoming a chef. These can coexist, but the released dialogue does not supply the connection. This is a supported text-level mismatch, not proof every viewer is confused.
4. **Narrator identity carries unexplained context.** The puppet shirt says **JARED**, while the spoken lines never identify that name or explain its role. Viewers can see a puppet and its operator; knowing who the named figure is requires additional context.
5. **Reveal and voice distinction were not separately demonstrated.** The first puppet frame exposes the holder; every speech event uses Dotter's voice; no actual listening confirmed a distinguishable narrator treatment. Frame evidence supports the premature visual disclosure. Metadata alone cannot establish how similar or different the performances sound.
6. **A large red oval puppet-mouth pose is ambiguous in isolated stills.** Earlier reviewers noted a possible nose-like reading at **31.25 seconds**. This remains a still-image interpretation to test in playback, not a proven motion defect.

The story-level omissions above deserve attention even when the export is valid and every required object exists. Adding labels or checking file integrity cannot establish the missing causal relationship.

## What I learned and carry-forward

- **Prior lesson applied only partly:** identity and computer use became explicit and a reveal was sampled, but the first reveal frame still disclosed the operator. The existence of a camera move is weaker evidence than the information change it was meant to create.
- **One supported lesson:** check what the released material actually establishes, rather than crediting an explanation supplied outside the film. Technical integrity, readable labels and a plausible interpretation are separate from demonstrated story communication.
- **Technical lesson:** a printed completion marker is not process termination or lock release. Sequential heavy phases must use actual completion and lock state.
- **Voice lesson:** uncertainty from ASR should remain uncertainty unless another modality resolves it. Correct written captions do not certify spoken words.
- **Process change supported by this record:** retain the explicit distinction between source facts, sampled observations, interpretation and unavailable review. The release record remains intact; this post-mortem does not silently replace its adverse evidence.

### One next experiment, proposed rather than run

Use this exact released MP4 as a frozen baseline for **one unprimed normal-speed, sound-on review**, without supplying this post-mortem or an intended answer. Keep the file and neutral questions fixed. Record the viewer's account of the character's goal, reason for choosing this activity, actual computer output, failed assumption and each speaker's role; request the film evidence for each answer. Record what the viewer knows at puppet entry and what changes after the widening.

The observable result is whether those facts are recovered from the film or require guessing, plus the actual playback/listening modality. Stop after the planned review and preserve uncertain/missing answers. If sound-on access fails, the result is an unresolved review gate, not a pass. This proposes an evidence-gathering method only; it discloses no upcoming story/hook and does not authorize a recut, new post or rule change.
