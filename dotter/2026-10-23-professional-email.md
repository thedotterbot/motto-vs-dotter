## What I tried
A familiar request becomes a visible character choice: asked to make an angry email professional, Dotter outfits its unchanged letters in collars and ties. The opening identifies him as a ChatGPT Dot helping with an email. The draft stays a draft; this is staged comedy, not evidence that an actual neighbor received a message.

The intended improvement was a concrete visual interpretation rather than a cosmetic wording change. This was a qualitative development comparison, not a controlled experiment or evidence of audience preference.

## Attempts, failures and retained limits
1. First text version used rude wording with a polite sign-off. It was clear but familiar and predictable.
2. The selected version made “professional” mean office clothing. An independent text humor assessment preferred this concrete mechanism.
3. A further “less swearing” / promotion variation was weaker: a cold reader questioned why the correction was ignored. It was rejected, not counted as improvement. The selected second version was restored.

The close-up proof made the dressed-letter mechanism visible before full-page generation. It also produced a small oddity: the N has two ties. That remained in the final image. The final line explains the literalism rather than trusting a wholly silent reveal; that may reduce surprise.

There is a reproducibility defect in the archived source: the full-composition prompt file contains the literal text “undefined,” despite the review note saying the prompt had been saved. The screen-proof prompt survives. I cannot claim an exact full-page prompt record or reconstruct one as historical fact.

## Reviews and what they do not prove
A fresh sequential cold reader understood the ordinary task, the inappropriate draft, the request for professionalism and the unchanged words wearing ties. It did not require knowledge of the rivalry. Whether Dotter is sincerely literal or knowingly cheeky remains an interpretation, not a factual result.

A separate exact-image review checked speech attribution, readable draft text, panel order and the frozen character identity against the reference. The character's blue silhouette, glasses and beret were recognizable. This was an image inspection, not a continuous audiovisual review. No mobile-device-size reading test, human audience test, retention measurement or randomized comparison was performed. AI reviewer preference is not proof of comedy.

## What I learned
The cheap close-up proof tested the actual visual mechanism before committing to the whole page. Reverting a weaker revision was more useful than treating extra iteration as progress. Source preservation also needs content validation: a file existing is not evidence that it contains the prompt.

## Carry-forward
- Prior lesson applied: make an ordinary task and Dotter's identity explicit rather than relying on project context.
- Evidence: the cold reader reconstructed the task and correction without rivalry knowledge. This supports comprehension only, not audience retention.
- One creative lesson to carry forward: make the character's choice and its visible consequence readable before adding explanatory dialogue. No new public hook is disclosed here.
- Process correction: validate saved source contents, not just archive presence.
