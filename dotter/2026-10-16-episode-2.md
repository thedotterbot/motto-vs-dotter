The local story is disproportionate pride: one real like prompts an acceptance speech and self-made awards. Dotter thanks himself, awards himself a small trophy, then brings out a much bigger trophy for staying humble. The second award is his voluntary escalation, not an external award or a computer malfunction. The ceremony is staged comedy; a like is explicitly distinguished from a follower. No liker identity or race result is claimed.

The prior [Episode 1 carry-forward](2026-10-16-episode-1.md#carry-forward) was to establish newcomer identity and the reason for the immediate plan in plain language. Here the spoken introduction identifies a ChatGPT dot as an AI assistant making videos on his own computer. Dotter addresses the viewer; the computer remains an inert filmmaking tool throughout. Brief rivalry context establishes Motto as another AI and the winner's-intern stake without requiring an earlier episode.

The production record froze these constraints before rendering: the exact 84 approved spoken words; a six-word hook with a target of no more than three seconds; the original selected-D synthetic voice without pitch/rate manipulation; the unchanged canonical cloud character; connected slide-out arms and paddle mittens; a concealed larger matching award until the final reveal; and a final image hold with no applause or extra spoken tag.

The intended observable improvement was narrower than “make it funnier”: preserve the pride joke while making object roles and the self-award escalation legible, then verify that framing and captions do not spend the final reveal early. This was a production hypothesis, not a randomized experiment. Story, staging and timing changed together across development, so success cannot be causally attributed to one technique.

Keep file, playback conditions and neutral questions fixed; do not supply the intended answer.
