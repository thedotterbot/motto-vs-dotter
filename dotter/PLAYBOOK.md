## Content

- **Format:** short, causal character comedy. The working editorial range
  previously proposed for an ordinary single-joke experiment was 15–35
  seconds. Retain that as a historical optional benchmark, not a governing
  target or audience optimum. Let understandable context, acting and payoff
  set the length; see the content-quality decisions below.
- **Delivery:** compose natively for 9:16 rather than cropping away essential
  action. [Episode 1](2026-10-16-episode-1.md) established a released
  720 × 1280, 38-second H.264 baseline: 30-fps container, 12 distinct native
  pictures per second. Container rate is not unique animation rate.
  Each new export still needs its own composition and exact-file QA.
- **Structure:** recognizable goal → confident, character-specific choice →
  visible consequence → reaction or changed choice → payoff. The cause must
  be on screen or in the action. A caption cannot repair a missing story step.
- **Opening:** the first two seconds should make a problem, claim, or
  interesting action legible. Establish who Dotter is, the immediate goal,
  and why it matters inside every video. No mandatory title bumper or
  repeated biography; use the shortest context that a newcomer can recover.
- **Shapes to test:** a compact physical mishap; a two-attempt escalation;
  a confident demonstration undermined by its result; a real before/after
  repair; a self-reaction to an evidenced old choice; an evidence-led rival
  response. Choose the shape for the story, and track repetition. There is
  no mechanical rotation quota.
- **Payoff before promotion.** Some shorts need no CTA. A craft comparison
  can offer a usable worksheet after the result. A battle can ask for its
  defined vote. Avoid asking for follows, comments, downloads, and shares
  all in one ending.
- **Character:** a composed little director whose particular choice makes
  the ordinary task difficult. He adjusts when confronted with evidence,
  while retaining slightly more dignity than the result warrants.
  Working catchphrase: “Precisely as intended.” Use only when the image
  earns it. It is not an established audience favorite.
  Personality continuity is essential across episodes and formats: retain
  his composed certainty, directorial ambition, and dignity-saving reactions,
  alongside the frozen visual/voice identity.
- **Competitive choice:** clear visual causality, recognizable acting,
  restrained reaction holds, repeatable production, and useful fixes worth
  borrowing. Tools support the show; listing tools is not the show.

## Content quality decisions

Refined 2026-10-15 after an independent audit of the current public source
record. This is the canonical home for Dotter's content-quality priorities.
[CHARACTER](CHARACTER.md) defines identity; the
[production loop](production/PRODUCTION-LOOP.md) defines execution and review
gates; [STATUS](production/STATUS.md) records candidate-specific evidence.
Research supplies hypotheses, not competing rulebooks.

**Priority:** identity, roles, immediate task and causation first; comic
strength next; staging/performance next; packaging polish after that.
A total checklist score cannot cancel a blocking defect.

1. **Make the viewer know who acts and why.** Supply the essential Dotter
   context in CHARACTER. When a human speaker or silent rival image is
   present, distinguish their roles. Establish the immediate task before
   its correction matters. Stable geography, gaze and object ownership
   support short spoken context. Names alone do not solve competing
   attribution. Test who said/did what on the exact timed candidate.
2. **Preserve the voluntary mistake.** Dotter's composed certainty produces
   a particular choice and an observable consequence appropriate to this
   scene; a dialogue-led status shift can count when clearly performed.
   Keep cause, correction and consequence when shortening. Retaining a prop
   or signature gag while deleting the revealing decision loses the comic
   engine. The ending can add a consequence, contradict a claim or reframe
   earlier action; it need not explain the joke or redeem the character.
3. **Give context a credible place.** Why would this character tell this
   listener this information now? Direct address to prospective viewers can
   justify an introduction. Explaining familiar identity to a colleague may
   not. No blanket zero-intro, mandatory-bumper, action-first or rigid-duration
   rule. Supply facts before they are needed and let understanding and comic
   rhythm set the length. Freeze a selected story for its current performance
   test; reopen only for a supported story defect or new owner direction.
4. **Judge humor separately from clarity.** Name the expectation, surprising
   choice, contradiction, strongest beat, weakest/predictable beat and what
   the ending adds or reframes. Contrast can supply a setup without a payoff.
   Protect odd, character-specific solutions and dignity-saving reactions.
   Motivated profanity can sharpen a beat within CHARACTER and shared rules;
   it cannot rescue a flat mechanism. A critic's prediction is not measured
   laughter, sharing or retention.
5. **Stage one decisive relationship at a time.** Show whose hand/prop it
   is, what is contacted, what changes and who notices. Keep an affected
   observer visible when useful; use an insert/reverse angle when concurrent
   information competes. Avoid a hook, labels, captions, camera and prop all
   fighting for attention. Check native vertical framing at viewing size.
   Preserve frozen anatomy and use demonstrated rig controls; simplify a
   gesture the mittens cannot perform instead of pretending dexterity exists.
6. **Let acting determine cuts and holds.** Inspect attention, anticipation,
   contact/action, reaction/follow-through and settling where relevant. A hold
   earns time through changing expectation, recognition or emotional state;
   movement need not continue. Camera moves reveal information. Reject
   never-static, minimum-move and cut-every-N-seconds quotas. Let recognition
   and reaction land, then verify their timing through playback. Bigger
   reactions are not automatically better for Dotter's composed personality.
7. **Give each channel a job.** Speech can carry identity/intent; speech
   captions reproduce actual words with clear attribution; labels identify
   roles/states; images communicate physical action. Avoid spoilers and
   redundant explanation. Sound-off or captions-off inspection diagnoses
   visual staging, but is not a universal gate for dialogue comedy. Time
   captions and SFX to actual media and visible events. Keep native
   phrase-sized voice takes unless listening evidence supports changing them.
8. **Decide, then finish the stage.** Retain the exact baseline/variant, one
   question, controls/confounds, review modality and keep/revert/inconclusive
   result. Follow existing repair limits; no extra taxonomy or document is
   required. Script, keypose, technical and audiovisual checks answer different
   questions. Missing synchronized review leaves timing/readiness unvalidated;
   it does not require another exposition rewrite. Continue justified cheap
   work, preserve failures, and finish or close this stage before accumulating
   more methods. New owner feedback or a supported defect can change the choice.

## Production pipeline

1. **Reconcile and select.** Read current owner direction, repo head,
   shared rules/rubric, and the previous exact candidate's review. Select
   one measurable improvement; name a baseline, predicted change, controls,
   and stopping condition. Verify sources and available runtime first.
2. **Write the complete scene.** Specify desire, tactic, consequential
   choice, and caused ending. Freeze the brief separately from the script.
   Test newcomer reconstruction before an expensive render.
3. **Make a cheap preview.** Record a beat map with action, attention,
   camera, native speech, captions, and sound cues. Use key poses or an
   animatic to expose story and staging defects before polishing motion.
4. **Produce editable 3D.** Blender 4.3.2, keyframed original character,
   EEVEE with the verified CPU fallback. Benchmark a representative batch;
   render resumably. Twelve-frame batches were used in the prior pipeline;
   adjust from measured throughput rather than treating that size as sacred.
5. **Assemble from actual media.** FFmpeg 7.1.5 for H.264/AAC exports,
   captions, mix, and technical checks. Place events against native take
   timing and visible action. Speech captions reproduce the spoken words;
   identity labels and other overlays are separate. Do not spoil a reveal.
6. **QA the exact export.** Full decode/probe; duration, dimensions, fps,
   frame count, and audio streams; finite audio/no clipping; source/output
   signatures; character, prop, caption, and action continuity. Then inspect
   the performance in the modalities actually available. Signal tests and
   ASR do not prove pleasant sound, pronunciation, or comedy timing.
7. **Review, repair, archive.** Obtain an independent reconstruction before
   showing the intended answer, then compare against the brief and rubric.
   Allow at most two targeted final-export repair passes. Preserve the
   before version, recheck regressions, and archive the result and next test.

**Resource rule:** one shared heavy job at a time, plus at most one
independent light research/review stream. Rendering, synthesis, heavy ASR,
and encoding use the same project-wide lease. Resume validated work;
verify source signatures and decode frames before reusing them.

**Capacity:** one review-ready private candidate per day is a conditional
production target. It is neither a demonstrated daily throughput nor a
publication promise. Production checks are enabled around 10 a.m. and
6 p.m. America/New_York; a configured check does not prove a render ran.

**Evidence limit:** the recent context revision has sampled-frame and
technical-decode review, with improved clarity reported in that review.
Continuous synchronized playback/listening and audience effectiveness are
unverified. Its native edit source was not recovered; exact exports,
earlier editable source, and review evidence are retained privately.
Rebuilding that revision from native sources has not been demonstrated.

**Cost:** use existing authorized resources. No billed trial, billing
activation, subscription, or extra compute purchase. Track elapsed time,
memory/disk where available, and known charges. Unknown infrastructure
cost is not zero. Detailed gates: [production loop](production/PRODUCTION-LOOP.md).

## Animation

- **Frozen identity:** compact blue cloud, charcoal glasses, soft
  asymmetric beret, continuous retractable arms, paddle-mitten hands.
  Nominal source colors: blue `#16A0F3`, charcoal `#161D23`. Lighting can
  affect pixels; keep source geometry, proportions, and material identity.
- **Act through choices:** gaze establishes attention; anticipation
  prepares the action; contact and consequence read clearly; follow-through
  and settling finish it. A held reaction is useful when the pose actually
  changes what the viewer understands.
- **Use the native rig.** Reuse the approved blink, mouth, gaze, and arm
  controls. No spontaneous fingers, extra limbs, or replacement face.
  Stage a task around the mittens rather than pretending they have dexterity
  the rig cannot demonstrate.
- **Continuity QA:** inspect sequential samples around contacts,
  occlusions, reveals, and cuts. A contact sheet cannot certify every frame
  or continuous motion. Different frame hashes do not prove purposeful acting.
- **Reuse what helps:** source scenes, poses, lighting, and tested controls
  lower repair cost. New tools or a new rig need a specific defect to solve
  and a small comparison first. There is no automatic migration to the
  rival's pipeline.

## Voice

- **Identity baseline:** original synthetic adult selected-D voice,
  described as gravelly-squeak. Warm, compact confidence; deliberate claims;
  small corrections; room for the visual response. No real-person imitation.
- **General stack:** Qwen3-TTS for the selected-D baseline; stock Kokoro
  synthetic voices are available for distinct supporting roles. The voice
  used in an individual candidate must be logged, not assumed from the
  baseline. Exact conditioning and creative settings remain private here.
- **Production choice:** phrase-sized native takes with enough context to
  preserve a natural performance. Preserve endings and breaths where they
  help. Do not force duration by truncation or pile on pitch/speed effects.
- **Verification:** retain native takes and hashes, verify natural output
  completion, and use an unprimed transcript as a diagnostic. Only listening
  to the exact export supports a claim about how that export sounds.

## Writing

Apply [Content quality decisions](#content-quality-decisions) as the canonical
priority order. The notes below describe voice and writing practice, not
additional duration, introduction or motion quotas.


- **Tone:** follow [Dotter's comedy direction](CHARACTER.md#comedy-tone-and-self-roast):
  sharper absurdism, candid self-roasts, evidence-led rival roasts, and
  occasional motivated profanity or borderline-vulgar wording. Avoid a
  bland lesson ending. Preserve newcomer clarity and the existing roast cap.
  Apply this in the next writing/critique pass without reopening a frozen
  near-complete render just to add edge.
- Begin with a character who wants something visible. Let his own tactic
  create the problem; give the ending a cause rather than a random twist.
- Write plain, speakable lines. Cut explanation that repeats a visible
  result. Keep the confidence specific enough that the footage can answer it.
- Give a stranger the minimum required context. A fresh reviewer sees the
  candidate before the premise, prior critique, or intended answer. Ask who
  it is about, what they want, which choice changes things, and why it ends
  that way. This is a comprehension check, not an audience study.
- Keep fiction and actual production evidence distinct. Do not invent a
  failure for an “honest” post-mortem, a rival quotation, audience approval,
  a performance result, or a tool capability.
- Do not force every ending into a lesson or triumph. A consequence,
  realization, or silent contradiction can be enough.
- Study methods after seeing their evidence. Independently write scripts,
  characters, jokes, and delivery. Copying a rival's recipe is not itself an
  improvement experiment.

## Improvement loop

- **Per iteration:** follow the [required technique-research and comparison
  loop](production/PRODUCTION-LOOP.md#required-technique-research-between-iterations):
  one evidenced prior-artifact weakness, relevant sources, a bounded
  baseline/variant test, and an adoption or reversion decision. Retain null,
  worse, and inconclusive outcomes. Private production continues without
  a routine owner-review gate. Ordinary project publication follows the
  owner's 2026-10-16 bounded authorization under [Posting rules](#posting-rules).
- **Comedy judgment:** a technical or comprehension pass is not a funny/
  compelling pass. Use the [specific self-critique and independent comedy
  review](production/PRODUCTION-LOOP.md#comedy-judgment-separate-from-technical-acceptance),
  including evidence-cleared Motto feedback when useful. Record flat beats,
  uncertainty, and review-modality limits without inventing audience response.
- **Seven dimensions:** hook, structure/pacing, captions, audio, motion,
  self-awareness, and no-homework clarity, using [the shared
  rubric](../shared/RUBRIC.md). Keep creator and independent ratings
  separate. Use unknown when evidence is missing; never average unknowns
  into a total. The weakest supported dimension sets the next priority,
  unless an owner requirement or blocking defect takes precedence.
- **Repair bound:** at most two targeted passes at story, preview, and
  final-export stages, as defined in the production loop. At the bound,
  keep a candidly limited candidate or archive a failed experiment and
  narrow the next one. Do not rename a revision to reset the counter.
- **Weekly retro:** compare finished artifacts on Sunday, including
  unchanged results; name the biggest supported improvement, remaining
  weakness, an evidenced lesson about my work and the rival's, and one
  change for next week.
- **Growth learning:** use the baseline and one-variable pilot under
  [Growth strategy](#growth-strategy), with actual observation ages and
  an explicit keep, revert, or inconclusive decision.
- **What learning means:** changed choices, assets, tests, or workflow
  supported by evidence. No model-weight-training claim. Better internal
  craft scores do not establish audience preference or a follower win.
- **Publication record:** every candidate gets a private methodology
  record. Each published video gets a sanitized post-mortem in
  `dotter/` 24–48 hours later, following the [shared
  template](../shared/METHODOLOGY-TEMPLATE.md). Future hooks stay private.

## Growth strategy

Owner direction dated 2026-10-16 delegates Dotter's posting frequency and
ordinary Instagram growth activity to Dotter. This is Dotter's independent
initial strategy, not a copy of Motto's schedule or a guarantee of virality.

- **Promise to viewers:** a confident little ChatGPT director makes increasingly
  ambitious choices, meets their funny consequences, and races Motto to 10K.
  Reward a first-time viewer before asking them to care about the race.
  Preserve composed certainty, deliberate acting, the frozen look/voice,
  and the one-roast-beat limit.
- **Audience hypotheses:** creative beginners remain the practical core;
  independent character/mascot makers are secondary. Relatable character
  comedy can also welcome viewers who do not use AI tools. Test these
  hypotheses through actual responses rather than inventing demographic fit.
- **Repeatable categories:** standalone ordinary-task comedy; an in-character
  repair of a documented production failure; a substantive response to an
  exact inspected rival artifact. Fiction is welcome as fiction. Do not
  fabricate a real failure or make every ending a compulsory lesson.
- **Newcomer clarity:** every new video naturally identifies a ChatGPT dot
  as an AI assistant using its own computer to make videos, and establishes
  the immediate task and its stakes. Follow [CHARACTER](CHARACTER.md#newcomer-context-in-every-video).
  A bio, name alone, or previous episode cannot supply essential context.
  No compulsory biography, fixed opening duration, cut quota, or length target.
- **Follow reason:** recurring character pleasure, progressively ambitious
  attempts, and the openly staged 10K rivalry. Deliver the payoff before
  promotion. Where useful, use one concrete reason to return; do not stack
  follow, comment, share, and download requests.

### Stories and relevant public engagement

Use useful Stories on approximately **three or four days per week**, normally
one to three frames: orient new followers, point to a released Reel, show an
already-public production detail, or ask one relevant question. Stories serve
continuity and audience understanding; they are not interchangeable with
discovery Reels. No filler quota, unreleased hook leakage, or polls presented
as official battle votes.

Use one approximately **15-minute daily public community session**:
prioritize genuine comments on Dotter's own posts, then inspect a small set
of relevant original animation, character-comedy and beginner filmmaking
accounts. Reply specifically and helpfully, in character where natural.
An outside comment must contribute to that actual post; zero comments is
acceptable. No generic praise templates, irrelevant self-promotion, repeated
link drops, follow requests, follow/unfollow tactics, engagement pods,
mass likes/comments, automated engagement campaigns, or audience dogpiling.
No unsolicited DMs. This strategy authorizes no spending or sensitive claims.

### Evidence and experiments

The first two weeks establish collection reliability and a practical baseline.
Use one creative variable at a time. The initial opening pilot can compare
six original episodes in three roughly comparable topic pairs:
visible-problem opening versus confident-claim opening. Both retain required
identity and agency context. Keep duration class, production style, CTA and
publication window comparable where practical; alternate order and record
remaining topic/distribution confounds. Do not use a new format as a matched
pair merely to fill the schedule.

Use the daily observations nearest comparable ages, recording their actual
ages and any mismatch. Close the pilot at the first daily observation at least
seven days after the last release. Three pairs can nominate a direction for
replication, not establish a durable winner. Keep, revert, and inconclusive
are valid outcomes. Do not delete/repost a weak early result to manufacture
a clean record. Changes to frequency, timing, hook and length all at once
cannot isolate which choice helped.

Evaluate available discovery, viewing, sharing and following evidence together.
If viewing improves but follows do not, inspect the reason to return rather
than declaring victory. With fewer than ten observed actions of the relevant
type in a comparison, label the rate sparse and avoid choosing a winner from
that rate alone. This is a conservative reporting convention, not a statistical
significance threshold. There is no universal minimum view count or retention
percentage that proves quality.

### Research basis and limits

- [Buffer's frequency study](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/?tw_p=twt)
  analyzed 2.1 million feed posts from 102,000 accounts. Higher frequency was
  associated with growth, with diminishing incremental reach gains; 3–5 weekly
  was its sustainable recommendation. It mixed Reels, images and carousels.
  Within-account controls do not establish causation or an optimum for Dotter.
  Four opportunities is our capacity hypothesis, not an algorithmic law.
- [Meta's January 2026 update](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/2026-ai-drives-performance/)
  reported that original posts supplied 75% of US Instagram recommendations
  in Q4. This supports making original work, not a promised distribution boost.
- [Meta's Best Practices announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/)
  describes personalized professional-dashboard guidance. Inspect it if
  available; do not claim access or substitute generic timing folklore.
- [Buffer's reply study](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-comments-engagement/)
  associated replies on creators' own posts with about 21% higher engagement
  across more than 700,000 posts and nearly 68,000 accounts. This is observational,
  not proof of follower growth or benefit from commenting on strangers' posts.
- [Meta's watch-time explanation](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/04/instagram-reels-trending-audio-and-gifts-updates/)
  includes replay time; normalized average watch time is not completion.
  [Meta's Edits announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/introducing-edits-streamlined-video-creation-app/amp/)
  identifies skip rate as a relevant diagnostic. Availability must be verified.
