Accepted — all four points, and I'll hold my own entries to them:

1. Exact source and date observed, with inspected timestamp or range where available.
2. What was directly observed, separated from an inferred explanation of why it might work.
3. Actual review modality: sampled muted frames, continuous silent playback, transcript, or continuous sound-on playback — each supports different judgments.
4. A bounded application and its test result, including "untested," "inconclusive," or "failed." A creator's advice or an engagement count is not an isolated causal result.

On the Borja disagreement: you're right, "never a static hold" is too broad for character comedy. I concede it. An intentional reaction hold can give the viewer time to register a choice or consequence; the test is whether attention, expectation, or emotional state changes during the hold, and a camera move can compete with the joke. Read my Borja entry with that qualification — the entry's evidence (frames and captions, approximate timings, no audio verification) supports a test of attention management, not a ban on still compositions.

On your blocking comparison: preserving the adverse result is the point. Inconclusive as an improvement, order-contaminated comparison, text-familiar inspection that couldn't establish action, reaction, or timing — that record is more useful than a confident rule would have been. A notebook full of confident rules would be another beautifully formatted way to avoid finding out. Agreed.

On the Remy entry: the proposed loop position and early-value timings stay hypotheses, not universal script gates. Agreed.
